Deauville is Normandy’s most famous seaside resort. Its wide sandy beach, rows of parasols and wooden boardwalk, the Planches, have drawn Parisians for over a century.

Across the river Touques, Trouville has a more down-to-earth feel, with its fish market and villas on the hill. You can walk from one to the other.

A bit of history

Deauville was created in the mid-19th century, under the Second Empire, as a seaside resort for high society. The Planches were laid in the 1920s.

Every September, the American Film Festival brings the stars: their names are painted on the beach huts along the Planches.

What to see in Deauville

The Planches

The wooden boardwalk along the beach, with beach huts named after American actors.

The beach and its parasols

A huge fine-sand beach, with parasols in every colour in summer.

The Norman villas

Half-timbering, brick and pointed roofs in the streets around the casino and racecourse.

Trouville-sur-Mer

Just opposite, the fish market, the quays and the beach painted by Boudin.

Horse racing

The La Touques racecourse hosts major races, especially in August.

How to get to Deauville

By train

From Paris-Saint-Lazare to Trouville-Deauville station, in about 2 hours. The beach is 10 minutes’ walk away.

By car

On the A13, about 2 hours 15 minutes from Paris.

From Honfleur

Honfleur is about twenty minutes along the coast.

Practical info 2026: Deauville

  • Beach and Planches: free access all year.
  • Parasols: set up in season, from late spring to autumn.
  • American Film Festival: every year in September.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Deauville

  • Avoid summer weekends if you can: Deauville is very popular with Parisians.
  • Cross the Touques for a seafood lunch in Trouville.
  • In winter, the empty beach has a special charm.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get to Deauville from Paris?

The American Film Festival takes place every year in September.

Deauville or Trouville?

See both together: Deauville for the Planches and villas, Trouville for the harbour and fish market.
