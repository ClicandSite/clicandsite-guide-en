Caen is the city of William the Conqueror, who built his castle and two great abbeys here in the 11th century. Badly hit in 1944, it is now home to the Memorial, one of the most visited museums in France outside the Paris region.

It is also a good base for the D-Day beaches and the Normandy coast.

A bit of history

Around 1060, William, Duke of Normandy, founded the castle. He also built the Men’s Abbey, where he is buried, while his wife Matilda founded the Ladies’ Abbey. In 1944 the Battle of Caen destroyed much of the city, which was rebuilt after the war.

What to see in Caen

The Caen Memorial

A museum on the Second World War, D-Day and the Cold War, built above a former German command post.

The castle

One of the largest fortified enclosures in Europe, with the Fine Arts Museum and the Normandy Museum.

The Men’s Abbey

Saint-Étienne church, William’s tomb, and the monastery buildings that house the town hall.

The Ladies’ Abbey

The Trinity church, founded by Queen Matilda.

How to get to Caen

By train

About two hours from Paris-Saint-Lazare.

By ferry

Ouistreham port, about fifteen kilometres away, receives ferries from England.

Getting around

The tram serves the centre; buses run to the Memorial.

Our tips for visiting Caen

  • Book your Memorial ticket online in summer.
  • Visit the Memorial before the D-Day beaches to understand the sites better.
  • The Memorial is not recommended for children under 10.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Caen Memorial cost?

€20.80 full price and €18.50 reduced in 2026.

How long does it take to visit the Memorial?

At least three hours; some visitors spend the whole day there.

What can you see in Caen in a day?

The Memorial in the morning, then the castle and the Men’s Abbey in the afternoon.
