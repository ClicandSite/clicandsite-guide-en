The cliffs of Étretat

White chalk arches plunging into the Channel and the Needle rising from the sea: the landscape painted by Monet and Courbet.

On the Alabaster Coast, about 30 km from Le Havre, Étretat sits between two chalk cliffs. To the west is the Aval cliff and its arch, the Porte d’Aval, which Maupassant likened to an elephant dipping its trunk in the sea; right beside it stands the Needle, an isolated stack about fifty metres high.

Claude Monet painted these cliffs from almost every angle in the 1880s. The clifftop paths are free and open all year, and give the best viewpoints.

What to see in Étretat

The Aval cliff

Climb the steps at the end of the beach for a view down onto the Porte d’Aval and the Needle.

The Amont cliff

On the other side, the chapel of Notre-Dame-de-la-Garde and a head-on view of the Needle.

The Manneporte

The largest arch, about a 30-minute walk beyond the Porte d’Aval.

Les Jardins d’Étretat

Contemporary gardens on the Amont cliff with views over the bay (paid entry).

How to get to Étretat

By car

About 2.5 hours from Paris. Paid car parks near the beach, free parking on the Criquetot road, 30 minutes’ walk away.

By coach

There is no station in Étretat: regular coaches run from Le Havre.

On a day trip

Day tours from Paris combine Étretat and Honfleur.

Tips for visiting Étretat

  • Arrive early: parking gets difficult by midday.
  • Stay on the paths and keep away from the edge and the foot of the cliffs: the chalk regularly collapses.
  • Tunnels such as the Trou à l’Homme are closed to the public.
  • Late afternoon gives the best light on the Porte d’Aval.

Frequently asked questions

Are the Étretat cliffs free?

Yes, the beach and the clifftop paths are free all year. Only the Jardins d’Étretat and parking have a fee.

Can I get to Étretat by train?

No, there is no station. Take the train to Le Havre, then a coach to Étretat.

How long do I need in Étretat?

Half a day is enough for both cliffs; allow a full day with the Manneporte and the gardens.
