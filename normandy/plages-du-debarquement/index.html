The D-Day beaches of Normandy

Omaha Beach, Pointe du Hoc, Arromanches and the American Cemetery: the places of 6 June 1944 remain among the most moving in France.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

On 6 June 1944, the Allies landed on five Normandy beaches: Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword. Museums, gun batteries, artificial harbours and cemeteries now line the coast of Calvados and the Manche.

The American Cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer, above Omaha Beach, holds 9,387 graves. Entry is free. To really understand the sites, a guided tour from Bayeux or Caen is well worth it.

What to see on the D-Day beaches

The American Cemetery

White crosses facing the sea, the chapel and the visitor centre with its free exhibition.

Omaha Beach

“Bloody Omaha”, the deadliest beach of D-Day, and its Les Braves memorial.

Pointe du Hoc

The cliffs scaled by the Rangers, still pitted with bomb craters.

Arromanches

The remains of the Mulberry artificial harbour, visible at low tide.

How to visit the D-Day beaches

From Bayeux

The best base: many guided minibus tours leave from the town centre.

From Paris

Day trips exist, but allow about three hours’ drive each way.

By car

Essential to explore independently: the sites are spread over about 100 km of coast.

Tips for the D-Day beaches

  • Pick one sector (American, or British and Canadian) rather than trying to see everything in a day.
  • The American Cemetery is open 9am to 5pm in winter and 9am to 6pm from April to September; it is closed on 1 January and 25 December.
  • The Bayeux Tapestry is on show at the British Museum in London until 11 July 2027, so it cannot be seen in Bayeux at the moment.
  • The Caen Memorial is a good complement to the beaches.

Frequently asked questions

Is the American Cemetery at Colleville free?

Yes, entry and the visitor centre are free, with no booking needed.

How many days do I need for the D-Day beaches?

One day is enough for the American sector (Omaha, Pointe du Hoc, the cemetery). Allow three days to see all five beaches.

Can I see the Bayeux Tapestry in 2026?

No, it is on loan to the British Museum until 11 July 2027 and the Bayeux museum is closed for works until its planned reopening in autumn 2027.
