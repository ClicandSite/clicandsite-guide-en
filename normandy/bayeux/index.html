Bayeux, the tapestry town

A Norman cathedral, an intact old town and the gateway to the D-Day beaches. The tapestry itself is in London until 2027.

Bayeux, in Calvados, is known worldwide for its tapestry, an embroidery nearly 70 metres long telling the story of William the Conqueror’s conquest of England in 1066.

Note: the Bayeux Tapestry museum has been closed since September 2025 for a major renovation, and the tapestry is on loan to the British Museum in London until 11 July 2027. The town is still an ideal stop for its cathedral and the D-Day beaches.

A bit of history

Bayeux was the first town in mainland France to be liberated in June 1944, and it was hardly bombed: its medieval centre survived intact.

The tapestry had left Normandy only twice since the 19th century. It is due to return to Bayeux in an enlarged museum, scheduled to reopen in 2027.

What to see in Bayeux

Notre-Dame cathedral

A Romanesque and Gothic cathedral consecrated in 1077, with its crypt and frescoes.

The old town

Half-timbered houses, mansions and mills along the river Aure.

Tapestry exhibitions

While the museum is closed, temporary exhibitions are shown in town, at the Baron-Gérard art and history museum and the Battle of Normandy memorial museum.

The Battle of Normandy memorial museum

The story of summer 1944, opposite the British war cemetery.

The D-Day beaches

Arromanches and Omaha Beach are about twenty minutes away: Bayeux is the starting point for many tours.

How to get to Bayeux

By train

From Paris-Saint-Lazare, via Caen, in just over 2 hours. The station is 15 minutes’ walk from the centre.

By car

On the N13, about 30 minutes from Caen.

To the beaches

Minibuses and guided tours leave Bayeux for the American and British beaches.

Practical info 2026: Bayeux

  • Bayeux Tapestry museum: closed for renovation; reopening planned in 2027.
  • Bayeux Tapestry: on show at the British Museum, London, from 10 September 2026 to 11 July 2027.
  • Cathedral: free entry.

Information checked in November 2026 with the French Ministry of Culture and the Bayeux museums; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Bayeux

  • If you are coming for the tapestry, check the museum’s reopening date before booking.
  • Stay overnight in Bayeux to head out early to the D-Day beaches.
  • In the evening, the floodlit cathedral is worth the walk.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see the Bayeux Tapestry in 2026?

Not in Bayeux: it is on show at the British Museum in London from 10 September 2026 to 11 July 2027, while the museum is renovated.

When does the Bayeux Tapestry museum reopen?

Reopening is planned in 2027.

Is Bayeux close to the D-Day beaches?

Yes, Arromanches and Omaha Beach are about twenty minutes by car.
