Port Hercule, between the Rock and Monte-Carlo

Yachts at the foot of the Rock, Grand Prix corners on the quays and a covered market where you lunch like the locals: Monaco’s harbour is the crossroads of the Principality.

Tucked between the Rock and Monte-Carlo, Port Hercule has been Monaco’s natural harbour since ancient times. Its quays host yachts of every size, with the La Condamine district just behind.

Every spring the quays become part of the Formula 1 Grand Prix circuit, and in September the Monaco Yacht Show brings together the finest boats in the world. The rest of the year people stroll here, swim in the Olympic pool in summer or skate in winter.

A bit of history

The port is named after the legend that Hercules passed through Monaco: in ancient times the place was already called the port of Hercules Monoikos. This natural cove, sheltered by the Rock, has been used as a harbour for centuries.

Its main breakwaters were built in the early 20th century, and a huge semi-floating dyke was added in 2002. Every year the Grand Prix circuit runs round the harbour.

La Condamine market

The covered market and its counters, where you can lunch on socca and local dishes.

How to get to Port Hercule

By train

Regional trains from Nice take about twenty minutes to Monaco-Monte-Carlo station, then free public lifts and escalators take over.

By car

There are plenty of public car parks but they are expensive; a chauffeur transfer saves looking for a space.

Practical info 2026: Port Hercule

  • Monaco is a sovereign state: there are no border checks from France and the euro is the currency, but carry photo ID.
  • Grand Prix and Yacht Show: during these events the quays are closed or ticketed and hotel prices soar.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Port Hercule

  • Lunch at La Condamine market, the most affordable spot in Monaco.
  • Walk up to the Rock by the Rampe Major for the view over the port.
  • Avoid Grand Prix dates if you want to stroll the quays.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Monaco Grand Prix go past the port?

Yes, the circuit runs along the quays of Port Hercule, with the swimming-pool corner and the chicane.

Where can you eat cheaply in Monaco?

At the covered market in La Condamine, just behind the port.

Can you swim at Port Hercule?

In the Rainier III Olympic pool, open to the public in summer.

How long should you spend at Port Hercule?

Allow two hours.

What is there to see near Port Hercule?

In this guide, the closest places are the Rock of Monaco (about 5 min on foot), Monaco (about 10 min on foot) and Monte-Carlo (about 15 min on foot).
