Port Lympia, sailors’ Nice

Colourful pointu boats, red Italian-style façades and busy quays: on the far side of Castle Hill, Nice’s port has kept its soul.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Dug in the 18th century, Port Lympia is framed by ochre and red Italian-style façades, with the columned front of Notre-Dame-du-Port at the end of the basin. Pointus, brightly coloured traditional fishing boats, sit alongside yachts and ferries to Corsica.

Quieter than Vieux-Nice, the district is known for its antique shops, bars and fish restaurants. It is also where boat trips leave for Villefranche bay, Cap Ferrat and Monaco.

A bit of history

Nice’s harbour was dug from 1749 on the orders of the King of Sardinia, who then ruled Nice, in a marsh fed by a clear spring, the “Lympia”, which gave it its name.

Place de l’Île-de-Beauté and its neoclassical façades have lined it since the 19th century. It still links Nice with Corsica.

The harbour mouth

The lighthouse and jetty, where you can watch the ferries leave for Corsica.

How to get to Port Lympia

By tram

Line 2, Port Lympia stop, the terminus on the port side.

On foot

About fifteen minutes from Vieux-Nice, following the seafront around Castle Hill.

Practical info 2026: Port Lympia

  • Boat trips: in season, excursion boats leave the port for Villefranche, Cap Ferrat and Monaco; book ahead in summer.
  • Ferries: Corsica ferries also leave from the port; avoid driving there at boarding times.

Tips for visiting Port Lympia

  • Come late in the day, when the façades glow red.
  • Rent an e-bike to ride up Mont Boron with no effort.
  • Sail to Villefranche-sur-Mer and its bay.

Frequently asked questions

What is a pointu?

A traditional Mediterranean wooden fishing boat, painted in bright colours.

Can you take a boat from Nice to Monaco?

Yes, in season excursion boats link the port of Nice with Monaco and Cap Ferrat.

How do you get to Port Lympia?

By tram, line 2, Port Lympia stop, or on foot from Vieux-Nice.

How long should you spend at Port Lympia?

Allow two hours, plus a boat trip.

What is there to see near Port Lympia?

In this guide, the closest places are Vieux-Nice (about 15 min on foot), Place Masséna and the Carré d’Or (about 20 min on foot) and Nice (about 25 min on foot).
