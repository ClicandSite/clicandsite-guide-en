Visit Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat

The billionaires’ peninsula, but also one of France’s most beautiful coastal paths and a pink villa surrounded by nine gardens.

Between Villefranche and Beaulieu, Cap Ferrat juts into the sea, covered in pines and villas hidden behind their gates. The village of Saint-Jean, its small harbour and restaurants keep a relaxed feel.

Most people come for two things: Villa Ephrussi de Rothschild and its gardens, and the path that circles the peninsula at the water’s edge.

Things to do in Cap Ferrat

Villa Ephrussi de Rothschild

A pink palace built for Baroness Béatrice de Rothschild in the early 20th century, with its art collections and nine themed gardens overlooking the sea on both sides.

The coastal path

The path circles the peninsula and Pointe Saint-Hospice. Coves, clear water and views of Monaco and Italy.

Saint-Jean harbour

A small fishing and pleasure harbour with waterside restaurants.

Paloma beach

A small sheltered beach, partly public, facing the bay of Beaulieu and the mountains.

Villa Kérylos, in Beaulieu

Next door in Beaulieu-sur-Mer, a recreation of an ancient Greek villa set on a rock by the water.

How to get to Cap Ferrat

By train, then on foot

Get off at Beaulieu-sur-Mer station: Villa Kérylos is close by, and Villa Ephrussi is about a twenty-minute uphill walk.

By bus

Buses link Nice with Cap Ferrat via Villefranche.

By car or with a driver

Parking is limited on the peninsula, especially in summer. A driver lets you combine the villas and Èze in one day.

Tips for visiting Cap Ferrat

  • Visit Villa Ephrussi in the morning, picnic on the peninsula and keep the afternoon for the path.
  • The coastal path is closed in rough seas: check the weather before you set off.
  • Bring a swimsuit: the coves along the path are made for swimming.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to visit Villa Ephrussi?

Allow about two hours for the villa and gardens, a little more if you linger in the tea room.

Is the Cap Ferrat coastal path difficult?

It is easy but long: allow several hours for the full loop. You can do just part of it, for example around Pointe Saint-Hospice.

Can you swim at Cap Ferrat?

Yes, at Paloma beach, Passable beach and many coves reached from the path.
