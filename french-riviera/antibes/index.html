Visit Antibes

Ramparts facing the sea, a Picasso museum in a château and one of the most beautiful coastal paths on the Riviera.

Photo: Jane Ackerley / Unsplash

  1. Home

Antibes has kept the soul of a small Provençal town, with its old quarter tucked behind the ramparts and its covered market. Port Vauban, one of Europe’s largest marinas, welcomes some of the world’s biggest yachts.

To the south, Cap d’Antibes unfolds its villas, pines and coves, with Juan-les-Pins on the other side, its sandy beach and its July jazz festival.

Things to do in Antibes

The Picasso Museum

Housed in the Château Grimaldi on the ramparts, where Picasso worked in 1946. The works from that period stayed here.

The Provençal market

Under the covered hall of Cours Masséna in the old town: local produce, olives, flowers and specialities in the morning.

The ramparts and old town

Walk along the sea-facing ramparts, then explore the lanes and the Safranier quarter, a village within the town.

Port Vauban and Fort Carré

The harbour and its giant yachts, overlooked by Fort Carré, a star-shaped fortress you can visit.

The Cap d’Antibes coastal path

A path carved into the rock by the sea, from Garoupe beach towards the tip of the cape. Wild scenery and turquoise water.

Juan-les-Pins

Sandy beaches, pine woods and a seaside-resort feel. The Jazz à Juan festival has been held here every summer since the 1960s.

How to get to Antibes

By train

Antibes station is on the coastal TER line, about ten minutes from Cannes and twenty from Nice. Juan-les-Pins has its own station.

By bus

Local buses link the old town, Cap d’Antibes and Juan-les-Pins. For the coastal path, get off near Garoupe beach.

Tips for visiting Antibes

  • Start with the Provençal market in the morning, then the Picasso Museum, before heading to the cape in the afternoon.
  • The Cap d’Antibes path is rugged: good shoes are essential, and it is closed in bad weather.
  • Antibes is halfway between Nice and Cannes: a good base for exploring by train.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Cap d’Antibes coastal path?

Allow about an hour and a half there and back for the most spectacular section, from Garoupe beach. The ground is uneven and can be slippery.

Antibes or Juan-les-Pins: where to stay?

Antibes for the charm of the old town and harbour; Juan-les-Pins for the sandy beach and summer evenings.

Is the Picasso Museum in Antibes worth it?

Yes, especially for the setting: a château by the sea, with a sculpture terrace overlooking the Mediterranean.
