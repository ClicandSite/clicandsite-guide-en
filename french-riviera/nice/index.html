Visit Nice

The capital of the Riviera: a huge bay, an old town of ochre façades and museums worthy of the greatest cities.

By Anthony, Cannes · Updated 12 November 2026

Nice is the gateway to the French Riviera: an airport minutes from the centre, a station with trains to Monaco, Antibes and Cannes, and a tram that links it all.

The city is best explored on foot, from the Promenade des Anglais to the lanes of the Old Town, then up to Castle Hill or the Cimiez district, where Matisse and Belle Époque high society lived.

Things to do in Nice

The Promenade des Anglais

Seven kilometres along the Baie des Anges, with its famous blue chairs. Walk, cycle or skate it, ideally at sunset.

Old Nice (Vieux-Nice)

Narrow lanes, ochre and red façades, baroque churches and little grocery shops. This is where you try socca, Nice’s chickpea pancake.

Cours Saleya

The flower and local produce market is held here on Tuesday to Sunday mornings; on Mondays it makes way for an antiques market.

Castle Hill

Nice’s most famous viewpoint, with its waterfall and a view over the whole bay and harbour. Go up by the steps or the lift.

The Matisse Museum

In a red Genoese villa in Cimiez surrounded by olive trees, a collection covering the painter’s whole career; he lived in Nice.

The Marc Chagall National Museum

Built to house the Biblical Message cycle, with stained glass and monumental canvases. One of the finest museums on the coast.

Port Lympia

Colourful façades and traditional fishing boats called pointus. The starting point for the walk to Rauba-Capeù point.

How to get to Nice

From the airport

Tram line 2 links Nice Côte d’Azur airport with the city centre and the harbour. It is the cheapest option.

By train

Nice-Ville station is served by TER trains on the coastal line (Cannes, Antibes, Monaco, Menton) and by TGV high-speed trains from Paris.

Getting around Nice

The centre is walkable; the tram and the Lignes d’Azur buses cover the rest. You do not need a car.

Tips for visiting Nice

  • Nice’s beaches are pebbly: water shoes make swimming much more pleasant.
  • Go up Castle Hill late in the afternoon for the light over the bay.
  • Nice is the best base for visiting Monaco, Èze, Villefranche or Saint-Paul-de-Vence without a car.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Nice?

Two days let you see the Old Town, Castle Hill, the Promenade and one or two museums. Add more if you use Nice as a base for the Riviera.

Are Nice’s beaches sandy?

No, they are pebbly. There are free public beaches and private beaches with sunbeds.

How do you get from Nice airport to the city centre?

The easiest way is tram line 2, which links the airport with the centre and the harbour. A taxi or private driver is easier with luggage.
