The Promenade Le Corbusier around Cap Martin

A 4.7 km path at the water’s edge, from Carnolès station to Roquebrune-Cap-Martin station, facing Monaco.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 15 November 2026

The Promenade Le Corbusier is the coastal path around Cap Martin, in Roquebrune-Cap-Martin. It links Carnolès station, on the edge of Menton, with Roquebrune-Cap-Martin station.

The path runs between the rocks and the gardens of grand villas, with the sea on one side and, at the end, a view of Monaco. It is an easy, free walk that you can do by train from end to end.

A bit of history

Since the 1980s the path has been named after the architect Le Corbusier, who spent his summers on Cap Martin. In 1952 he had his cabanon built there, a small wooden cabin by the water.

Since 2016 the cabin has been one of the Le Corbusier sites on the Unesco World Heritage list. It stands next to Villa E-1027 by the designer Eileen Gray. Le Corbusier died in 1965 while swimming below the cape.

The route

  1. Start at Carnolès station. Walk down to the seafront and on to the tip of Cap Martin.
  2. The path follows the coast between the rocks and the villa walls, all the way round the cape.
  3. The last stretch runs along the bay of Roquebrune facing Monaco, passes under the railway and ends near the Cap Moderne site and Roquebrune-Cap-Martin station.

Allow about 4.7 km one way, or 1 hour 30 minutes at an easy pace. The path is easy, with a few steps. Come back by train, from one station to the other.

What to see on the walk

The tip of Cap Martin

Flat rocks and clear-water coves, with a view of Menton and the Italian coast.

The villas of the cape

The path runs past estates hidden among pines and olive trees, once home to royalty and artists.

Cap Moderne

At the end of the walk, Le Corbusier’s cabin, Eileen Gray’s Villa E-1027, the Étoile de Mer restaurant and the camping units. They can only be visited with a guide.

How to get to the start

By train

TER trains on the Nice – Ventimiglia line stop at Carnolès and at Roquebrune-Cap-Martin: start from one station and come back from the other.

By car

Parking is scarce on the cape. The easiest is to park near one of the two stations.

Practical info 2026: Promenade Le Corbusier

  • Path: free, open all year, closed in bad weather and rough seas.
  • Cap Moderne: guided visits only, booking required, from April to early November: €19 adults, €10 ages 7 to 18, free under 7; the visit lasts about 2 hours.
  • Cap Moderne reception: on the forecourt of Roquebrune-Cap-Martin station.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with Cap Moderne (Centre des monuments nationaux) and the tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for the walk

  • Do not take the path in heavy swell: it is closed and dangerous.
  • Bring a swimsuit: several coves are good for a swim.
  • Book Cap Moderne ahead if you want to see the cabin: groups are limited to 12 people.
  • Walk from Carnolès to Roquebrune so that Monaco is in front of you.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Promenade Le Corbusier?

About 4.7 km between Carnolès station and Roquebrune-Cap-Martin station, or 1 hour 30 minutes on foot.

Can you visit Le Corbusier’s cabin?

Yes, with the Cap Moderne site, on a guided visit booked ahead, from April to early November. Adult entry is €19.

Is the path open all year?

Yes, but it is closed in bad weather and rough seas.

What is there to see near the Promenade Le Corbusier?

In this guide, the closest places are Roquebrune-Cap-Martin (right there), Menton (about 10 min by car) and Monaco (about 15 min by car).
