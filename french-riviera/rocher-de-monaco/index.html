The Rock, Monaco’s historic heart

A palace flying the princely flag, the changing of the guard at five to twelve and a museum clinging to the cliff: on its rock, Monaco-Ville tells seven centuries of Grimaldi history.

Monaco-Ville sits on the Rock, a headland sixty metres above the sea. This is where the Grimaldis settled at the end of the 13th century, and where the palace of the princely family still stands.

Its pedestrian lanes link Palace Square, the cathedral and the Oceanographic Museum, built on the cliff edge in the early 20th century. The Saint-Martin gardens, clinging to the rock, give spectacular sea views.

A bit of history

The Genoese built a fortress on the Rock in the late 12th century. In 1297 François Grimaldi took it by trickery, disguised as a monk: the Prince’s Palace was built on that fortress, and the Grimaldi family still lives there.

The Oceanographic Museum, founded by Prince Albert I, opened in 1910. Princess Grace and Prince Rainier III are buried in the cathedral.

Things to see on the Rock

The Prince’s Palace

The State Apartments, open to visitors in spring and summer. Book a guided tour of the Rock and the Prince’s Palace

The changing of the guard

Every day at 11.55 am on Palace Square, overlooking Port Hercule.

The cathedral

The white-stone cathedral where Princess Grace and Prince Rainier III are buried.

The Oceanographic Museum

The museum and aquarium built on the cliff, once run by Jacques Cousteau.

The Saint-Martin gardens

Shady paths along the cliff edge, facing the sea.

How to get to the Rock

By train

Regional trains from Nice take about twenty minutes to Monaco-Monte-Carlo station, then free public lifts and escalators take over.

By car

There are plenty of public car parks but they are expensive; a chauffeur transfer saves looking for a space.

Once there

From the port, walk up the Rampe Major, or take the bus to Place de la Visitation.

Practical info 2026: The Rock of Monaco

  • Monaco is a sovereign state: there are no border checks from France and the euro is the currency, but carry photo ID.
  • Prince’s Palace: in 2026 the State Apartments were open from 30 March to 15 October (€10 adults, €5 for ages 6-17 and students); they are closed until spring 2027. The changing of the guard, at 11.55 am on Palace Square, can be seen all year round.
  • Oceanographic Museum: open daily except the Grand Prix weekend and 25 December; 10 am to 6 pm from January to March and October to December. Ticket prices vary with the season; check them on the museum’s website.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Rock of Monaco

  • Arrive around 11.30 am to get a good spot for the changing of the guard.
  • Allow two hours for the Oceanographic Museum.
  • Walk down to Port Hercule by the Rampe Major.

Frequently asked questions

What time is the changing of the guard in Monaco?

Every day at 11.55 am on Palace Square.

Where is Grace Kelly buried?

In Monaco Cathedral, on the Rock, beside Prince Rainier III.

Can you visit the Prince’s Palace?

Yes, the State Apartments are open in spring and summer.

How long should you spend on the Rock?

Allow half a day.
