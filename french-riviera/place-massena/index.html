Place Masséna and the Carré d’Or

Black-and-white chequerboard paving, red arcaded façades and statues that light up at night: Place Masséna is the heart of Nice, between the old town and the Belle Époque districts.

Lined with red Genoese-style arcaded buildings, Place Masséna links Vieux-Nice with the modern city. Its chequerboard paving, the Fontaine du Soleil and Jaume Plensa’s seven seated statues, perched on poles and lit up at night, make it Nice’s favourite meeting point.

To the west lies the Carré d’Or, an upmarket district of luxury shops and Belle Époque buildings, which continues into the Musicians’ Quarter and its ornate façades. To the north, the Promenade du Paillon stretches out as a green corridor to the modern art museum.

A bit of history

Place Masséna was laid out in the 19th century over the Paillon, the river that separated the old town from the new districts and was gradually covered over. Its red arcaded façades follow the Turin style, since Nice was then ruled by Savoy.

It is named after Marshal André Masséna, born in Nice in 1758. Since 2007 Jaume Plensa’s illuminated statues have watched over the square, crossed by the tramway.

Things to see around Place Masséna

Place Masséna

The chequerboard, the red arcades and Jaume Plensa’s statues, which change colour at night. Book a guided tour of Belle Époque Nice

Fontaine du Soleil

The statue of Apollo and its fountain, at the south end of the square.

Promenade du Paillon

The big urban park, with its water mirror and play areas, between the square and MAMAC.

The Carré d’Or

Upmarket shopping streets and Belle Époque buildings between the square and the Negresco.

The Musicians’ Quarter

Ornate façades and 19th-century villas on streets named after Verdi, Rossini and Berlioz.

Avenue Jean-Médecin

Nice’s main shopping street, served by the tram.

How to get to Place Masséna

By tram

Line 1, Masséna stop; line 2 stops nearby at Jardin Albert-Ier.

On foot

Five minutes from Vieux-Nice or the Promenade des Anglais.

Practical info 2026: Place Masséna and the Carré d’Or

  • Water mirror: on the Promenade du Paillon, the fountains mostly run in the warmer months.
  • Christmas market: in December the square and gardens host the festive season celebrations.

Tips for visiting Place Masséna and the Carré d’Or

  • Come back after dark to see the statues lit up.
  • Use the square as your starting point for a day trip to Èze and Monaco.
  • Wander the Musicians’ Quarter for its Belle Époque façades.

Frequently asked questions

What are the statues on Place Masséna?

A work by Jaume Plensa, “Conversation in Nice”: seven figures seated on poles, lit up at night.

What is the Carré d’Or?

Nice’s upmarket district between Place Masséna and the Negresco, with luxury shops and Belle Époque buildings.

How do you get to Place Masséna?

By tram, line 1, Masséna stop.

How long should you spend around Place Masséna?

Allow two hours.
