Hyères, east of Toulon, is one of the oldest winter resorts on the French Riviera. It is also called Hyères-les-Palmiers: the town planted palm trees everywhere in the 19th century.

Above all, it is the gateway to the Golden Isles: Porquerolles from La Tour Fondue, at the tip of the Giens peninsula, and Port-Cros from the port of Hyères.

A bit of history

In the 19th century Hyères drew European aristocrats for its mild winters; Queen Victoria stayed there in 1892. In the 1920s the Noailles couple built a modernist villa on the hill, now an art centre.

What to see in Hyères

The old town

Medieval lanes, Place Massillon and a fortified gate, up to the ruins of the Château Saint-Bernard.

Villa Noailles

A 1920s modernist villa, now an art centre, set in a park with sea views.

The Giens peninsula

Joined to the mainland by two strips of sand, with the Pesquiers salt marshes and lagoon in between, home to pink flamingos.

Almanarre beach

A long beach on the western strip, a hotspot for kitesurfing and windsurfing.

The Golden Isles

Porquerolles 20 minutes by boat from La Tour Fondue, Port-Cros about an hour from the port of Hyères.

How to get to Hyères

By train

Hyères has a station, about twenty minutes from Toulon.

By plane

Toulon-Hyères airport is a few minutes from the centre.

By car

Via the A57 then the A570 from Toulon. In summer, Mistral buses link the town, the beaches and the ports.

Practical info 2026: Hyères

  • Old town and beaches: free access.
  • Villa Noailles: art centre; check the villa’s website for opening times and exhibitions.
  • Boats to the islands: all year with TLV-TVM: Porquerolles from La Tour Fondue, Port-Cros and Le Levant from the port of Hyères.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Hyères

  • In summer, book your crossing to Porquerolles: visitor numbers are capped.
  • Walk up to Villa Noailles late in the day for the view over the bay.
  • Come and see the flamingos in the salt marshes in spring or autumn.

Frequently asked questions

Where do you catch the boat to Porquerolles in Hyères?

At La Tour Fondue, at the tip of the Giens peninsula. The crossing takes about 20 minutes.

Where do you catch the boat to Port-Cros?

From the port of Hyères (Port Saint-Pierre), all year; the crossing takes about an hour.

What can you see in Hyères in a day?

The old town, Villa Noailles and the Giens peninsula with its salt marshes.
