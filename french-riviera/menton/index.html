Visit Menton

The pearl of France, right next to Italy: the mildest town on the coast, with colourful façades, lemons and gardens.

Menton is the last French town before Italy. Its climate, the mildest on the Riviera, lets lemon trees and exotic plants thrive, and draws visitors from the end of winter.

The old town climbs in steps up to the basilica, above the harbour and beaches. Italy is minutes away: Ventimiglia and its market are easy to reach by train.

Things to do in Menton

The old town

Yellow, orange and pink houses packed on the hill, stepped lanes and vaulted passages. The view from the harbour is the image of Menton.

The Saint-Michel-Archange basilica

A baroque church at the top of a grand staircase, with a pebble-mosaic forecourt. Chamber music concerts are held here in summer.

The Jean Cocteau Museum

A contemporary building on the seafront devoted to Cocteau, who loved Menton. The small Bastion museum on the harbour is dedicated to him too.

The gardens

Menton is a town of gardens: Serre de la Madone, Val Rahmeh, Fontana Rosa… Plants from all over the world enjoy the climate.

The covered market

Menton’s market hall, near the old town, for local specialities and the famous lemons.

How to get to Menton

By train

Menton is on the coastal TER line, about forty minutes from Nice via Monaco. Menton-Garavan station is closest to the old town.

By bus

Regional buses link Nice and Menton along the Lower Corniche, stopping in Monaco.

Tips for visiting Menton

  • Come in February for the Fête du Citron: giant sculptures made of citrus fruit fill the town. Book accommodation early.
  • Climb to the basilica through the old town’s steps, then go back down to the harbour on the other side.
  • Combine Menton with Roquebrune-Cap-Martin or a hop over to Italy.

Frequently asked questions

When is the Menton Lemon Festival?

Every February for about two weeks, with parades and citrus sculptures in the Biovès gardens.

Can you go to Italy from Menton?

Yes, by train: Ventimiglia is minutes away, and the line continues to Sanremo.

Does Menton have beaches?

Yes, several pebble and mixed sand-and-pebble beaches on either side of the old town.
