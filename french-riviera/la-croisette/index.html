La Croisette, the boulevard of cinema

Almost three kilometres of palm trees, beaches and grand hotels facing the bay of Cannes, from the Palais des Festivals to Pointe Croisette.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

The Croisette runs along the bay of Cannes for almost three kilometres, from the Old Port and the Palais des Festivals to Pointe Croisette. On one side are the sea and the public and private beaches; on the other, Belle Époque and 1920s grand hotels, luxury shops and the famous blue chairs.

This is where the Cannes Film Festival rolls out its red carpet every May. The rest of the year the promenade is still the heart of town: people stroll here at sunset, lunch with their feet in the sand, and catch boats to the Lérins Islands from the port next door.

A bit of history

La Croisette is named after a small cross that stood at the tip of the cape. Once a simple seaside track, it was laid out as a promenade in the 19th century when winter visitors arrived.

The grand hotels were built in the early 20th century: the Carlton opened in 1911, the Majestic and the Martinez in the 1920s. The first festival palace also stood here, before the current one.

Things to see on the Croisette

The Palais des Festivals

The famous festival steps, which you can climb for a photo, with the marina right beside them. Book a guided tour of Cannes and the Croisette

The Walk of Fame

Handprints of actors and directors set into the pavement around the Palais.

The grand hotels

The domed Carlton, the Art Deco Martinez and the Majestic, legendary festival party venues.

The beaches

Free public beaches and private beaches with sunloungers and restaurants all along the boulevard.

Pointe Croisette

At the far end, the quieter Palm Beach area and Port Pierre-Canto, with views of the Esterel.

Out at sea

From the nearby port, ferries reach Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands in about fifteen minutes.

How to get to the Croisette

By train

Cannes station is a five-minute walk from the Croisette, with frequent regional trains from Nice and Antibes and TGVs from Paris.

From Nice airport

About 30 km by motorway; allow 35 minutes to over an hour depending on traffic, more in summer and during the festival.

Practical info 2026: La Croisette

  • Works on the Croisette: the boulevard is being redeveloped in stages from 2026 until around 2030. Traffic and parking may be disrupted: come by train (the station is a five-minute walk away) or use the car parks in the town centre. The easiest option is to book a private chauffeur transfer, which drops you at your hotel door with no parking to find.
  • Cannes Film Festival: in May the area around the Palais is closed to the public and hotels fill up fast; book very early or avoid those dates.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Croisette

  • Come at sunset, when the light over the Esterel is at its best.
  • For lunch on the sand, book your private beach the day before in summer.
  • Then climb the lanes of Le Suquet, old Cannes, for the view over the bay.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Croisette?

Almost three kilometres, from the Palais des Festivals to Pointe Croisette.

Are the beaches on the Croisette free?

Some are: free public beaches alternate with paying private beaches.

Can you climb the steps of the Palais des Festivals?

Yes, outside events the steps are open for photos.

How long should you spend on the Croisette?

Allow half a day.

What is there to see near the Croisette?

In this guide, the closest places are Palm Beach (about 15 min on foot), Cannes (about 25 min on foot) and Le Suquet (about 25 min on foot).
