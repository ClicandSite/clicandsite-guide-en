Visit Saint-Paul-de-Vence

A walled village on its hill, art galleries at every door, and one of Europe’s finest modern art museums a short walk away.

Photo: V2F / Unsplash

Saint-Paul-de-Vence is the Riviera’s artists’ village. Its 16th-century ramparts still enclose a maze of cobbled lanes, fountains and galleries.

Chagall lived here and is buried in the small village cemetery. Just below, the Fondation Maeght, opened in the 1960s, brings together Miró, Giacometti, Braque and Chagall in a building designed for them.

Things to do in Saint-Paul-de-Vence

The ramparts walk

A walkway runs all the way round the village, with views over the hills to the sea on one side and the foothills of the Alps on the other.

Rue Grande and the galleries

The main street crosses the village from gate to gate. Painters, sculptors and ceramicists exhibit all year round.

The Fondation Maeght

A few minutes’ walk from the village, a museum in the middle of nature: the Giacometti courtyard, the Miró labyrinth and excellent temporary exhibitions.

Marc Chagall’s grave

In the cemetery at the southern end of the village. Visitors leave small pebbles, following tradition.

The pétanque square

At the village entrance, under the plane trees, where people have always played pétanque. A simple, very Provençal moment.

How to get to Saint-Paul-de-Vence

By bus from Nice

Regional buses link Nice and Saint-Paul-de-Vence, stopping at the foot of the village. Allow about an hour.

By car or with a driver

The village is pedestrianised; paying car parks surround it and are often full in summer. Saint-Paul combines easily with Vence or Grasse.

Tips for visiting Saint-Paul-de-Vence

  • Visit the Fondation Maeght in the morning and the village late in the day, when the light turns the ramparts golden and the groups have gone.
  • The lanes are cobbled and steep: flat shoes recommended.
  • Most galleries sell original works; you are welcome to go in and look without buying.

Frequently asked questions

Is Saint-Paul-de-Vence wheelchair accessible?

The village is steep and cobbled, so access is difficult. The Fondation Maeght is easier to get around.

Do you need to book the Fondation Maeght?

Not usually, but it is advisable in high season and during major exhibitions.

Can you combine Saint-Paul-de-Vence and Èze in one day?

Yes, by car or with a driver. Without a car it is harder: the two villages are on different bus lines from Nice.
