The Matisse Museum occupies the Villa des Arènes, a red Genoese villa in the Cimiez gardens, planted with century-old olive trees. Henri Matisse lived in Nice from 1917 until his death in 1954.

Opened in 1963, the museum holds the gifts made to the city by the artist and his heirs: nearly 600 works and objects that belonged to him. It traces the whole development of his art.

A bit of history

Henri Matisse settled in Nice in 1917 and lived there until his death in 1954. He spent his last years on Cimiez hill, in the former Hôtel Régina, and is buried in the nearby monastery cemetery.

The museum opened in 1963 in the Villa des Arènes, a 17th-century Genoese villa. It houses the gifts of the artist and his heirs to the City of Nice.

Things to see at the Matisse Museum

The collections

Paintings, drawings, prints, paper cut-outs and sculptures, from his early years to his last. See tickets for the Matisse Museum

The artist’s objects

Furniture and objects from his studio, which appear in his paintings.

The Arènes garden

An olive grove around the museum, with the remains of the Roman town.

Cimiez monastery

Right next door; Matisse is buried in its cemetery.

The former Hôtel Régina

The great Belle Époque palace where Matisse lived, visible from the garden.

How to get to the Matisse Museum

By bus

Lines 5, 18 and others, Arènes - Musée Matisse stop.

By car

Parking in the streets around the monastery.

With a chauffeur

From Nice airport, allow about 25 minutes depending on traffic.

Practical info 2026: Matisse Museum, Nice

  • Opening times: daily except Tuesdays; 10 am to 6 pm April to October and 10 am to 5 pm November to March. Ticket office closes 30 minutes earlier. Closed 1 January, Easter Sunday, 1 May and 25 December.
  • Price: €12 entry.
  • Address: 164 avenue des Arènes de Cimiez, in the Arènes garden.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Matisse Museum

  • Don’t come on a Tuesday, the closing day.
  • Combine with the Chagall Museum at the foot of Cimiez hill.
  • Take a break in the olive grove of the Arènes garden.
