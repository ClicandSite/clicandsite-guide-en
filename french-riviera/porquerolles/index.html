Visit Porquerolles

A car-free island with white-sand beaches and Caribbean-clear water, in the heart of a national park.

Porquerolles is the largest of the Îles d’Hyères, off the Giens peninsula. It is part of Port-Cros National Park: no cars, cycle paths, forests and some of the most beautiful beaches in France.

Explore the island by bike or on foot from the village and its harbour. To protect it, visitor numbers are capped on the busiest summer days.

Things to do on Porquerolles

Notre-Dame beach

A long fine-sand beach lined with pines, often listed among the best in Europe. Just under an hour on foot or a quarter of an hour by bike from the village.

Argent beach

Closer to the village, with pale sand and transparent water.

Fort Sainte-Agathe

The fort above the harbour, with a view over the whole island and the mainland.

Villa Carmignac

A contemporary art foundation in a villa among the vines. You visit barefoot; booking is recommended.

The cycle paths

A network of forest tracks links beaches, the lighthouse and coves. Bike hire in the village.

How to get to Porquerolles

By boat from La Tour Fondue

Shuttles leave from La Tour Fondue harbour at the tip of the Giens peninsula all year round. The crossing takes about twenty minutes.

Book in summer

In July-August visitor numbers are capped at around 6,000 a day: book your crossing in advance.

From other ports

In season, boats also leave from Hyères, Toulon and Le Lavandou.

Practical info 2026: Porquerolles

  • Boat: TLV from La Tour Fondue harbour (Giens peninsula, Hyères), a crossing of about 20 minutes.
  • Fares: adult return €17.70 from November to March, €24 from May to September; adult bike supplement €17 return.
  • Parking: the Tour Fondue car park is paid; alternatively use the free Arromanches car park and bus 67 (Mistral network) to the pier, no bikes on board.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with tourist offices and the sites themselves; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Porquerolles

  • Hire a bike as soon as you arrive and head for Notre-Dame beach before the crowds.
  • Bring plenty of water: the tracks get very hot in summer.
  • Paths may be closed when the fire risk is high.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to book the ferry to Porquerolles?

In July and August it is strongly recommended: visitor numbers are capped at around 6,000 a day.

Can you take a car to Porquerolles?

No, visitors’ cars are not allowed on the island. Leave yours at La Tour Fondue car park.

How long is the crossing to Porquerolles?

About twenty minutes from La Tour Fondue on the Giens peninsula.
