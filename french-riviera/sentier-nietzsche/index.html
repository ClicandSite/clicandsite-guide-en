The Nietzsche Path, from the sea to Èze village

A 2.1 km climb from Èze-sur-Mer station to the hilltop village of Èze, on the path the philosopher used to take.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 15 November 2026

The Nietzsche Path links the seafront at Èze-sur-Mer with the hilltop village of Èze, 400 metres higher. It zigzags up a valley of pines and rocks.

It is the most spectacular way to reach Èze: on foot, from the sea, with the village appearing above you as you climb.

A bit of history

The philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche stayed in Nice in the 1880s. He often walked up this path, and it was here that he conceived the third part of “Thus Spoke Zarathustra”. He came back every year until 1888.

The route

  1. Start at Èze-sur-Mer station. Walk east along the lower corniche road for about 200 metres to the trail sign.
  2. The path zigzags up the valley between pines and olive trees, with the sea behind you.
  3. You arrive at the foot of Èze village, near the entrance to the old town and the exotic garden.

Allow about 1 hour 30 minutes up for 2.1 km and 400 metres of climb, and 45 minutes down. The path is steep and stony. For a longer loop, the Sentier David continues to Mont Bastide (3 hours 30 minutes in all, about 590 metres of climb).

What to see on the hike

Èze village

A medieval eagle’s nest 400 metres above the sea, with its lanes and the exotic garden at the top.

The sea view

At each bend, the view opens onto the bay of Èze and Cap Ferrat.

The valley

The path crosses a wild valley of pines, holm oaks and rocks.

How to get to the start

By train

TER trains on the Nice – Ventimiglia line stop at Èze-sur-Mer, 200 metres from the start.

By bus

To come back from the village, buses link Èze-Village with Nice; you can also walk back down.

Practical info 2026: Nietzsche Path

  • Access: free trail, open all year.
  • Èze exotic garden: paid entry, at the top of the village.
  • Difficulty: steep, stony climb; no water on the way.

Information checked in November 2026 with the Èze tourist office; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for the hike

  • Walk up rather than down: the way down is slippery.
  • Start early in summer, the path is in full sun in the morning.
  • Take water and good shoes.
  • Combine the climb with a visit to the village and the exotic garden.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to walk up the Nietzsche Path?

About 1 hour 30 minutes for 2.1 km and 400 metres of climb. Coming down takes about 45 minutes.

Where does the Nietzsche Path start?

At Èze-sur-Mer, 200 metres east of the station, on the lower corniche road.

Why is it called the Nietzsche Path?

The philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche walked it in the 1880s; he conceived part of “Thus Spoke Zarathustra” there.

What is there to see near the Nietzsche Path?

In this guide, the closest places are Èze village (at the finish), Beaulieu-sur-Mer (about 10 min by car) and Plage de la Mala (about 10 min by car).
