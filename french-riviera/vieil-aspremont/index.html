The ruins of Vieil Aspremont

A short climb from the village of Aspremont to the ruins of a hilltop village abandoned in the 15th century.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 15 November 2026

Above the village of Aspremont, some twenty kilometres north of Nice, a hill carries the ruins of the first village, abandoned in the Middle Ages.

The climb is short but steep. At the top you find the remains of the chapel, an arch and stone walls, and a view from the lower Var valley to the peaks of the Mercantour.

A bit of history

The old village of Aspremont stood on the hilltop. It was abandoned in 1426 and the people settled lower down, where the present village is.

The ruins have been studied by archaeologists. They keep the remains of a chapel and an arch.

The route

  1. Start from the Saint-Claude church in the village of Aspremont.
  2. Follow the GR 5 uphill, then a steep, unmarked path to the top of the hill.
  3. Come back down the same way.

Allow about 2 hours for 3.6 km out and back with 310 metres of climb. The high point is at 813 metres. The end of the climb is steep and stony.

What to see on the hike

The ruins of the old village

Stone walls, an arch and the remains of the chapel among the vegetation.

The view over the Var and the Mercantour

From the top, the view drops onto the lower Var valley and runs up to the mountains.

The village of Aspremont

At the start, a hilltop village with concentric lanes around its church.

How to get to the start

By car

Aspremont is about 30 minutes from Nice via the Saint-Pancrace road. Park in the village.

By bus

Lignes d’Azur buses link Nice and Aspremont; check the timetable before you go.

Practical info 2026: Vieil Aspremont

  • Access: free, open all year.
  • Site: unrestored ruins: do not climb on the walls.
  • Waymarks: GR 5 at first, then an unmarked path.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for the hike

  • Wear good shoes: the end of the climb is steep.
  • Take water, there is none at the top.
  • Come on a clear day to enjoy the view to the Alps.
  • Do not move the stones: it is an archaeological site.
