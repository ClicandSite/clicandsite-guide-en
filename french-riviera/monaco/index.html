Visit Monaco

Two square kilometres, a palace, a casino and one of Europe’s finest sea museums. Monaco is easy to see on foot in a day.

The Principality is best seen in two parts: the Rock, the old town on its clifftop with the Prince’s Palace and the Oceanographic Museum, then Monte-Carlo with its casino and gardens. Port Hercule and its yachts lie in between.

Monaco is a country in its own right, but there are no border checks coming from France and the currency is the euro. The town is steep: free public lifts link the different levels, so look for them before you tackle the stairs.

Things to do in Monaco

The Prince’s Palace and the changing of the guard

Every day at 11:55, the Prince’s Carabiniers change the guard in front of the Palace. Arrive a quarter of an hour early for a front-row spot. The State Apartments are open for part of the year.

The Oceanographic Museum

Founded by Prince Albert I and built into the cliff, it combines a remarkable aquarium with scientific collections. The rooftop terrace has one of the best views on the coast.

Monaco Cathedral

A white-stone church on the Rock, where Princess Grace and Prince Rainier III are buried. Entry is free outside services.

The Saint-Martin Gardens

A shady clifftop walk between the museum and the cathedral, ideal for a break from the heat.

Monte-Carlo and Casino Square

The casino, the opera house and the Hôtel de Paris form the most photographed corner of the Principality. The casino atrium can be visited during the day; smart dress is expected.

Port Hercule

The heart of the Formula 1 Grand Prix circuit, at the foot of the Rock. Walk along the quays to see the size of the yachts.

How to get to Monaco

By train

Monaco-Monte-Carlo station is served by regional TER trains on the coastal line: about twenty minutes from Nice, around an hour from Cannes. It is the easiest option.

By bus

Regional buses run along the Lower Corniche from Nice. Slower than the train, but with sea views all the way.

By car or with a driver

There are plenty of public car parks, all paying. During the Grand Prix and big events traffic is very difficult: take the train or a private transfer.

Tips for visiting Monaco

  • Start with the Rock in the morning (changing of the guard, museum), go down to the harbour for lunch, then up to Monte-Carlo in the afternoon.
  • On cruise-ship days the Rock is very busy between 10am and 3pm: the museum is the best place to be at those times.
  • Dress modestly for the cathedral and the casino.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a passport to go to Monaco?

There are no border checks between France and Monaco, but carry an ID document as you would anywhere when travelling.

How long do you need to visit Monaco?

One day is enough for the Rock, the harbour and Monte-Carlo. Allow an extra half-day to take your time at the Oceanographic Museum.

What is the best way to get from Nice to Monaco?

The TER train: about twenty minutes, to a station in the heart of the Principality. Driving is not recommended on busy days.
