Discovering the Esterel Massif

Red rocks plunging into a turquoise sea: between Cannes and Saint-Raphaël, the wildest landscape on the French Riviera.

The Esterel is a volcanic massif whose rock, rhyolite, gives it its striking red colour. It stretches between Mandelieu and Saint-Raphaël, across the Var and the Alpes-Maritimes.

Discover it by car along the Corniche d’Or, on foot along its trails, or from the sea, where the red coves are most spectacular.

A bit of history

The Esterel’s red rocks are volcanic: rhyolites formed about 250 million years ago. Covered in hard-to-reach forest, the massif long sheltered bandits, like the famous Gaspard de Besse in the 18th century.

The Corniche d’Or road, opened in 1903 with support from the Touring Club de France, made this coastline accessible to travellers.

Things to do in the Esterel

The Corniche d’Or

The coast road between Mandelieu-la-Napoule and Saint-Raphaël, cut into the red rock above the sea. One of the most beautiful roads in France. Book a guided tour or activity in the Esterel

Pic de l’Ours

One of the massif’s summits, with a view over the whole coast to Cannes and the Alps.

Cap Roux

One of the most spectacular headlands, reached by a hike from the Corniche.

The coves of Anthéor and Le Trayas

Small coves between the red rocks, with very clear water.

Agay beaches

A sheltered bay at the foot of the red Rastel d’Agay rock.

How to get to the Esterel

By car or with a driver

The Corniche d’Or is best driven, with plenty of places to stop. Allow an hour from Cannes with stops.

By train

TER trains between Cannes and Saint-Raphaël stop at a few small stations in the massif, such as Agay and Le Trayas, starting points for hikes.

Practical info 2026: The Esterel

  • By train: TER trains stop at Agay and Le Trayas; a marked loop leaves Le Trayas station for the Pic de l’Ours (492 m).
  • By car: from the Agay roundabout follow “Massif de l’Estérel”, then “Pic de l’Ours” to the Col Notre-Dame car park.
  • Fire risk: from late June to late September, access to the massif depends on the prefecture’s daily risk map and can be banned. No usual restriction in winter.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with tourist offices and the sites themselves; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Esterel

  • In summer, access to the massif depends on the fire risk and can be banned on some days: check the map published every evening by the Var prefecture before you go.
  • The massif is closed at night all year round. Set off early, with water and sun protection.
  • To see the coves at their best, take a boat trip from Cannes or Saint-Raphaël.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Esterel Massif open in summer?

From 21 June to 30 September, access depends on the day’s fire risk and can be banned in strong wind or heat. Check the prefecture’s map the day before.

Why are the Esterel rocks red?

The massif is volcanic: its main rock, rhyolite, has that distinctive red colour.

Can you visit the Esterel without a car?

Yes, by train to Agay or Le Trayas, where trails start. The Corniche viewpoints are easier by car.

How long should you spend in the Esterel?

Allow one day.

What is there to see near the Esterel?

In this guide, the closest places are Le Suquet (about 15 km away), Cannes (about 15 km away) and Lérins Islands (about 15 km away).
