Visit Èze

A medieval village clinging to its rock more than 400 metres above the sea. The view from the garden at the very top is worth the climb on its own.

Photo: Anatoly Volochay / Unsplash

Èze is one of the most spectacular hilltop villages on the Riviera, between Nice and Monaco. Cobbled lanes, vaulted passages, stone houses: you enter on foot and cars stay below.

At the top, on the ruins of the old castle, the Jardin d’Èze mixes succulents and sculptures, with a view over the Cap-Ferrat peninsula and, on very clear days, all the way to Corsica.

Things to do in Èze

The Jardin d’Èze

The highest point of the village, with a breathtaking view of the Mediterranean. It is the only paying part of the visit, and the one not to miss.

The village lanes

No map needed: climb towards the garden and get lost in the passages on purpose. Art galleries and small shops at every corner.

Notre-Dame-de-l’Assomption church

An ochre façade and a baroque interior in the heart of the village.

The Nietzsche path

The trail linking the seafront (Èze-sur-Mer) to the village, once walked by the philosopher. Allow a good hour of steep climbing: if you are not a walker, do it downhill.

The perfumeries

At the foot of the village, Grasse perfume houses have workshops and shops. Factory tours are free.

How to get to Èze

By bus from Nice

Regional buses climb the Middle Corniche and stop at the foot of the village. It is the most practical option without a car.

By train, then on foot

TER trains stop at Èze-sur-Mer, by the sea. From there the Nietzsche path climbs up to the village: a beautiful but demanding hike.

By car or with a driver

Parking at the foot of the village is scarce in season. A private driver makes it easy to combine Èze and Monaco in the same day.

Tips for visiting Èze

  • Arrive before 10am: coach tours arrive later and the lanes get crowded.
  • The streets are steep and cobbled: good shoes are essential.
  • Èze combines very well with Monaco, a few minutes’ drive away.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have to pay to visit Èze village?

No, the village is free to enter. Only the Jardin d’Èze at the top charges admission.

Can you visit Èze with a pushchair or wheelchair?

It is very difficult: the lanes are steep, cobbled and often stepped. A baby carrier is better.

How long does the Nietzsche path take?

About an hour uphill from Èze-sur-Mer, a little less going down. The path is steep and has little shade: avoid the hottest hours.
