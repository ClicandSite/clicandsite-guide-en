How to pay less for your lift pass

  • Buy online in advance: in Chamonix, a day starts at €49 online instead of €76.50; in Cauterets, some days are up to 40% cheaper online.
  • Ski on Saturday: it is changeover day, so passes are cheaper. In the 3 Vallées, a day is 20% off on Saturdays online; in Châtel it drops to €44; in Les Menuires, to €41.
  • Go early or late in the season: €52 in Megève instead of €65, €54.50 in Les 2 Alpes instead of €68, €66.10 in Val Thorens instead of €73.50.
  • Get a resort card: at the Grand Tourmalet, the No Souci card brings a day down to €38.50 outside school holidays.
  • Start on the beginner slopes: at Isola 2000, the front de neige pass is free.

The Pyrenees are cheaper than the Alps

The five Pyrenees resorts in the comparison all cost between €46 and €55 a day. In the Alps, you need to head to smaller resorts or the Southern Alps, such as Isola 2000, Auron or the Dévoluy, to find these prices. The big Savoie resorts go above €70.

The big linked ski areas

Passes covering several resorts cost the most: €84.20 for the 3 Vallées (Courchevel, Méribel, Les Menuires, Val Thorens), €78 for Tignes-Val d’Isère, €78 for Paradiski (La Plagne and Les Arcs) and €69 for the Portes du Soleil from Avoriaz or Châtel. If you only ski a little, a single-resort pass is often enough.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the cheapest ski resort in France in 2026-2027?

In our comparison, Isola 2000 and Auron, at €44 for an adult day pass, then Avoriaz at €44.50 on its own ski area. In the Pyrenees, Cauterets costs €46.

How much is a 3 Vallées lift pass in 2026-2027?

€84.20 a day for an adult in high season, €75.70 early and late in the season, and €421 for 6 days.

Are lift passes free for children?

At most big resorts the pass is free for under-5s, for example in Les Arcs, La Plagne and Les Menuires; in Tignes and Val d’Isère it is free for under-8s.
