Château de Chambord

The largest château in the Loire Valley, commissioned by Francis I from 1519, with its double-helix staircase and roof terraces bristling with chimneys.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Chambord is the most spectacular château in the Loire Valley: 426 rooms, 282 fireplaces and a walled forest estate, the largest in Europe. The grounds are free to enter all year round; only the château itself is ticketed.

From 26 October to 18 December the château opens 9 am to 5 pm, then 9 am to 6 pm over the Christmas holidays. From 28 November 2026 to 17 January 2027, “Christmas at Chambord” decorates the rooms on a Gargantua theme. The château is closed on 25 December and 1 January.

What to see at Chambord

The double-helix staircase

Two flights that spiral around each other without ever meeting, often linked to Leonardo da Vinci’s influence.

The roof terraces

A forest of chimneys, lanterns and turrets at the top, with views over the estate.

The royal apartments

The furnished and restored apartments of Francis I and Louis XIV.

The estate

Formal French gardens and 5,440 hectares of forest to explore on foot, by bike or by electric cart.

How to get to Chambord

By car

About 2 hours from Paris on the A10, exit Mer or Blois. Paid parking at the estate entrance.

By train

Train to Blois (1 h 30 from Paris-Austerlitz), then a shuttle or taxi to the château.

On a tour

Day trips from Paris combine Chambord, Chenonceau and a third château.

Our tips for visiting Chambord

  • Head up to the terraces first, before the tour groups arrive in late morning.
  • Take the HistoPad tablet: it shows the rooms as they looked in the Renaissance.
  • The grounds are free: allow time for a walk along the Cosson.
  • Book online during Christmas at Chambord, as weekends get very busy.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a ticket to Château de Chambord?

In 2026 a ticket costs €21 for residents of the European Economic Area and €31 for other visitors, €18.50 reduced. Entry is free for under-18s and for EU residents aged 18 to 25. The grounds are free.

What are Chambord’s winter opening hours?

From 26 October to 18 December, 9 am to 5 pm; from 19 December to 2 January, 9 am to 6 pm. Last entry 30 minutes before closing. Closed 25 December and 1 January.

Can you visit Chambord as a day trip from Paris?

Yes: allow about 2 hours by car, or take the train to Blois and then a shuttle. Organised day trips often combine Chambord and Chenonceau.
