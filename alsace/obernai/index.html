Obernai sits at the foot of the Vosges, about thirty kilometres south-west of Strasbourg. Its market square, lined with half-timbered houses, is one of the finest in Alsace.

The town is linked to Saint Odile, patron saint of Alsace, born in the area according to tradition. Mont Sainte-Odile and its convent overlook the plain a few kilometres away.

A bit of history

A free city of the Holy Roman Empire in the Middle Ages, Obernai belonged to the Decapolis, an alliance of ten Alsace towns. It has kept part of its ramparts, its corn hall and its belfry, the Kapellturm.

What to see in Obernai

The market square

The heart of the town, with the town hall, the corn hall and the Saint Odile fountain.

The Kapellturm

The old chapel bell tower, now a belfry, easy to spot with its four turrets.

The ramparts

A walk runs along the old fortifications and their towers.

Mont Sainte-Odile

The convent perched on the mountain, with a view over the whole Alsace plain.

How to get to Obernai

By train

Regional trains link Strasbourg and Obernai in about thirty minutes.

By car

About 30 minutes from Strasbourg on the motorway.

On a tour

Tours from Strasbourg combine Obernai, Mont Sainte-Odile and the Wine Route.

Practical information 2026: Obernai

  • Access: the old town is free to explore.
  • Market: the weekly market is held on Thursday mornings.
  • Christmas: Obernai has its own Christmas market, quieter than those in Strasbourg and Colmar.

Our tips for visiting Obernai

  • Go up Mont Sainte-Odile late in the day for the light over the plain.
  • Park outside the centre: the old town streets are narrow.
  • Combine Obernai with Strasbourg on the same day, by train.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get to Obernai from Strasbourg?

By regional train in about thirty minutes, or by car in around 30 minutes.

What can you see in Obernai in a day?

The market square, the belfry and the ramparts, then nearby Mont Sainte-Odile.

Is there a Christmas market in Obernai?

Yes, the town holds its own Christmas market every year in the historic centre.
