Saint-Michel: the gateway to the Latin Quarter

A fountain Parisians have used as a meeting point since 1860, medieval lanes, a legendary bookshop and Notre-Dame right across the river: Saint-Michel is postcard Paris on foot.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

The Saint-Michel area sits on the Left Bank, facing the Île de la Cité, around the square and boulevard of the same name. It is the way into the Latin Quarter: within a few minutes’ walk you go from the riverside booksellers to the medieval lanes around the church of Saint-Séverin.

The landmark is the Saint-Michel fountain, unveiled in 1860, where the archangel defeats the devil between two bronze dragons. All of Paris meets here. Notre-Dame is five minutes away, across the Petit-Pont.

A bit of history

Place and Boulevard Saint-Michel were created under Napoleon III during Haussmann’s great works. The fountain by Gabriel Davioud was unveiled in 1860: it shows Saint Michael slaying the dragon.

The area saw fierce fighting during the liberation of Paris in August 1944, as plaques on the façades recall, and later the barricades of May 1968.

Things to see in Saint-Michel

The Saint-Michel fountain

The huge monumental fountain on the square, with Saint Michael and two water-spouting dragons. The best-known meeting point on the Left Bank. Book a guided tour or activity in Saint-Michel

Rue de la Huchette

One of the oldest streets in Paris, now very touristy. At no. 5, the Caveau de la Huchette has been a jazz club since 1946.

Shakespeare and Company

The famous English-language bookshop facing Notre-Dame. Go in and browse; the queue is usually shorter in the morning.

Square René-Viviani

A small garden with the best view of Notre-Dame and the oldest tree in Paris, a locust tree planted around 1601.

The church of Saint-Séverin

A Flamboyant Gothic church with an ambulatory like a forest of stone palm trees, in the heart of the lanes.

The Musée de Cluny

The national museum of the Middle Ages, in a Gothic mansion and Gallo-Roman baths. Home to The Lady and the Unicorn tapestries.

How to get to Saint-Michel

By metro and RER

Saint-Michel station (line 4) and Saint-Michel–Notre-Dame (RER B and C), with a direct link to Charles de Gaulle airport.

On foot

From Notre-Dame, cross the Petit-Pont: two minutes to Rue de la Huchette.

Practical info 2026: Saint-Michel

  • Musée de Cluny (28 rue du Sommerard): closed on Mondays; permanent collections free on the first Sunday of the month.
  • Notre-Dame: entry to the cathedral is free, and booking a time slot on the official website is recommended.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Saint-Michel

  • Come early for Shakespeare and Company and Square Viviani, before the tour groups arrive.
  • Skip the restaurants with touts on Rue de la Huchette; head up towards Rue Saint-André-des-Arts instead.
  • Carry on to the Sainte-Chapelle and the Conciergerie, five minutes away across the Seine.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Saint-Michel fountain?

On Place Saint-Michel in the 6th arrondissement, where Boulevard Saint-Michel meets the Seine, facing the Île de la Cité.

How do I get to Saint-Michel from the airport?

RER B runs directly from Charles de Gaulle airport to Saint-Michel–Notre-Dame.

What is there to see around Saint-Michel?

Rue de la Huchette, Shakespeare and Company, Square Viviani, Saint-Séverin, the Musée de Cluny and Notre-Dame across the river.

How long should you spend in Saint-Michel?

Allow two to three hours.

What is there to see near Saint-Michel?

In this guide, the closest places are Sainte-Chapelle (about 5 min on foot), the Conciergerie (about 5 min on foot) and Notre-Dame (about 5 min on foot).
