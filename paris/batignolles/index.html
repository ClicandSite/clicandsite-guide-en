Batignolles, a village within Paris

A former village absorbed into Paris in 1860, with its church square, romantic garden and markets, now extended by one of the city’s newest neighbourhoods.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Batignolles, in the 17th arrondissement, was an independent village until it became part of Paris in 1860. It has kept a village feel: a small square around the church of Sainte-Marie, shopping streets and café terraces filled mostly with Parisians.

To the north-west, former railway land has become the Clichy-Batignolles eco-district, with its large Martin Luther King park and the new Paris courthouse designed by Renzo Piano. Two faces of the same area, ten minutes’ walk apart.

A bit of history

Batignolles was a separate village until 1860, when Paris expanded and absorbed it. In the 1860s Manet and his painter friends met at the Café Guerbois, so often that people spoke of the “Batignolles group”, the future core of Impressionism.

The Square des Batignolles dates from the same period. More recently, old railway land became the Martin-Luther-King park and Renzo Piano’s new Paris courthouse, opened in 2018.

Things to see in Batignolles

Square des Batignolles

An English-style garden with a stream, a pond, ducks and tall trees, a stone’s throw from the church. Book a guided tour or activity in Batignolles

Sainte-Marie des Batignolles church

A church with a columned portico on a little square that gives the area its village air.

Rue des Moines and Rue des Dames

The local shopping streets, with bakeries, cheese shops, wine merchants and small restaurants.

The Batignolles covered market

A neighbourhood covered market on Rue Lemercier, with fresh produce stalls.

Clichy-Batignolles–Martin Luther King park

A large new park with pools, lawns and playgrounds, among the contemporary buildings of the eco-district.

How to get to Batignolles

By metro

Rome (line 2) or Brochant (line 13) for the village, Pont-Cardinet (line 14) for Martin Luther King park.

On foot

Montmartre and Pigalle are a quarter of an hour away via Place de Clichy.

Practical info 2026: Batignolles

  • Batignolles organic market: Boulevard des Batignolles, Saturday mornings.
  • Square des Batignolles and Martin Luther King park: free, during Paris city garden opening hours.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Batignolles

  • Come on Saturday morning for the organic market on the boulevard, then have lunch on a terrace on Rue des Moines.
  • Do a loop: Square des Batignolles, the church square, then Martin Luther King park via Rue Cardinet.
  • Combine with Montmartre in the afternoon, heading up via Place de Clichy.
