Père Lachaise Cemetery and its famous graves

Jim Morrison, Oscar Wilde, Édith Piaf, Chopin: the world’s most famous cemetery is also a vast romantic park, free and open every day.

Covering 44 hectares with more than 70,000 graves, Père Lachaise is the largest cemetery in Paris and the most visited in the world. You wander it like a park, along cobbled lanes lined with chapels and funerary sculpture.

Entry is free. The cemetery is so large that it is worth having a map or joining a guided tour to find the best-known graves without going round in circles.

A bit of history

The cemetery opened in 1804 on land that had belonged to Father de La Chaise, Louis XIV’s confessor. Considered too far from the centre, it drew few burials at first.

To make it popular, the city moved the presumed remains of Molière and La Fontaine there in 1817, along with those of Héloïse and Abélard, and it worked at once. The Communards’ Wall recalls the execution of Commune fighters in May 1871.

What to see at Père Lachaise

Jim Morrison

The Doors singer’s grave is still one of the most visited, always covered in flowers. Book tickets or a guided tour at Père Lachaise

Oscar Wilde

His sculpted monument, now behind glass, is covered in messages from admirers.

Édith Piaf and Chopin

Two musical pilgrimages, alongside Molière, Proust and many others.

Héloïse and Abélard

The neo-Gothic tomb of the Middle Ages’ most famous lovers.

How to get to Père Lachaise

By metro

Line 2 or 3 to Père Lachaise, for the Boulevard de Ménilmontant entrance.

From the top

Line 3 to Gambetta: enter at the top of the cemetery and walk gently downhill.

Main entrance

Boulevard de Ménilmontant, in the 20th arrondissement.

Practical info 2026: Père Lachaise

  • Entry: free, every day.
  • Winter hours (6 November – 15 March): 8am–5:30pm on weekdays, 8:30am–5:30pm on Saturdays, 9am–5pm on Sundays and public holidays. Fountains are shut in winter.
  • Access: main gate on boulevard de Ménilmontant (métro Philippe-Auguste, line 2); Porte des Amandiers opposite Père-Lachaise métro (lines 2 and 3); Porte Gambetta (métro Gambetta, line 3).

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Père Lachaise

  • Start at the Gambetta entrance so you walk down rather than up.
  • Download a map before you go or join a two-hour guided tour.
  • Wear good shoes: the lanes are cobbled and hilly.
  • Be respectful: it is a working cemetery with funerals every day.

Frequently asked questions

Is Père Lachaise free?

Yes, entry is free and needs no booking. Only guided tours have a fee, from about €20.

What are the opening hours?

The cemetery is open every day, usually from 8am to 6pm on weekdays, opening later at weekends and closing earlier in winter. Check paris.fr before you go.

Where is Jim Morrison’s grave?

In Division 6, fairly close to the main entrance. A map or guide will save you time.

How long should you spend at Père Lachaise?

Allow 1.5 to 3 hours.

What is there to see near Père Lachaise?

In this guide, the closest places are Belleville (about 25 min on foot), Bastille (about 2 km away) and Le Marais (about 3 km away).
