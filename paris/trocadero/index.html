The Trocadéro, facing the Eiffel Tower

The esplanade of the Palais de Chaillot gives you the most famous photo in Paris: the whole Eiffel Tower, across the Seine. Around it, gardens, museums and an aquarium.

The Trocadéro is the Chaillot hill on the Right Bank, directly opposite the Eiffel Tower. The Palais de Chaillot, built for the 1937 International Exhibition, spreads its two curved wings either side of a large esplanade lined with gilded statues.

From there, gardens slope down to the Seine around the Warsaw fountain, and the Pont d’Iéna leads to the foot of the tower. It is the most photographed viewpoint in Paris, by day and by night.

A bit of history

The name recalls the Battle of Trocadero, a fort near Cadiz taken by the French army in 1823. A first Trocadéro palace was built for the 1878 World’s Fair, then replaced by the Palais de Chaillot for the 1937 Exhibition.

At the Palais de Chaillot the UN General Assembly adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights on 10 December 1948; the esplanade is now called the Parvis des Droits de l’Homme.

Things to see at the Trocadéro

The Trocadéro esplanade

The Parvis des Droits de l’Homme, between the two wings of the palace: a head-on view of the Eiffel Tower, with its gilded statues. Book a guided tour or activity at the Trocadéro

The Trocadéro gardens

Sloping lawns, pools and the great Warsaw fountain, with its water jets facing the tower.

The Cité de l’Architecture et du Patrimoine

Life-size casts of cathedral portals and murals, and a fine view from the galleries.

The Musée de l’Homme

The museum of human evolution and societies, in the Passy wing of the palace.

The Musée National de la Marine

Model ships, paintings and maritime objects, in a fully renovated museum.

The Paris Aquarium

Dug into old quarries beneath the gardens, with sharks and jellyfish; a family favourite.

How to get to the Trocadéro

By metro

Trocadéro station (lines 6 and 9), which opens straight onto the esplanade.

On foot

From the Eiffel Tower, cross the Pont d’Iéna and walk up through the gardens: about ten minutes.

Practical info 2026: Trocadéro

  • Light show: every evening the Eiffel Tower sparkles for five minutes on the hour, from nightfall.
  • Palais de Chaillot museums: most are closed on Tuesdays; check their websites before you go.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Trocadéro

  • Come at sunrise to have the esplanade almost to yourself, or at dusk to watch the tower light up.
  • Watch out for pickpockets and pushy sellers on the esplanade, especially in the evening.
  • Carry on with a Seine cruise: boats leave from the foot of the Eiffel Tower, right opposite.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best view of the Eiffel Tower?

From the Trocadéro esplanade, between the two wings of the Palais de Chaillot, and from the gardens below.

What time does the Eiffel Tower sparkle?

Every evening, for five minutes on the hour, once night has fallen.

Is the Trocadéro free?

Yes, the esplanade and gardens are free; only the museums and the aquarium charge admission.

How long should you spend at the Trocadéro?

Allow an hour, plus museums.

What is there to see near the Trocadéro?

In this guide, the closest places are Eiffel Tower (about 10 min on foot), Seine cruise (about 15 min on foot) and Crazy Horse (about 20 min on foot).
