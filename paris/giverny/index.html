Giverny, in Monet’s garden

The village where Claude Monet lived for over forty years and painted his Water Lilies: his green-shuttered house, his flower garden and the famous pond with its Japanese bridge.

By Anthony, Cannes · Updated 12 November 2026

Giverny is a small Normandy village on the Seine, about 75 kilometres from Paris. Claude Monet moved here in 1883 and stayed until his death on 5 December 1926. He created two gardens: the Clos Normand in front of the house, and the water garden with its water lily pond and Japanese bridge.

The house and gardens can only be visited during the warmer months. In 2026, the centenary of his death, Paris is also honouring the painter: from autumn 2026 to spring 2027, the Musée d’Orsay and the Musée de l’Orangerie are running a special programme, perfect while you wait for Giverny to reopen.

Things to see in Giverny

Monet’s house

The pink house with green shutters, its yellow dining room, blue-tiled kitchen and collection of Japanese prints.

The Clos Normand

The flower garden in front of the house, with its paths, rose arches and beds that change colour through the seasons.

The water garden

The water lily pond, the Japanese bridge and the weeping willows, just as Monet painted them.

The Musée des Impressionnismes

A museum devoted to Impressionism and its heirs, two minutes’ walk from the house.

Sainte-Radegonde church

The little village church, where Monet is buried with his family.

How to get to Giverny

By train

From Paris Saint-Lazare to Vernon-Giverny, then shuttle bus, bus or bike for the few kilometres to the village.

On a tour

Day trips leave from Paris, often combined with Versailles or Rouen.

Practical info 2026: Giverny

  • Monet’s house and gardens: open in 2026 from 1 April to 1 November, daily; closed in winter, reopening expected in spring 2027.
  • Monet centenary in Paris: from autumn 2026 to spring 2027, special programme at the Musée d’Orsay and the Musée de l’Orangerie.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Giverny

  • Book online and arrive at opening time, 10am, or in the late afternoon to avoid the groups.
  • To see the water lilies in bloom, come between June and September; in May it is irises and wisteria.
  • This winter, see the great Water Lilies at the Orangerie in Paris while you wait for the reopening.

Frequently asked questions

When can you visit Monet’s gardens at Giverny?

Only in the warmer months: in 2026, from 1 April to 1 November. They are closed in winter.

How do I get to Giverny from Paris?

By train from Paris Saint-Lazare to Vernon-Giverny, then shuttle, bus or bike to the village; or on an organised day trip.

Where can I see Monet’s Water Lilies in winter?

At the Musée de l’Orangerie in Paris, which displays the great Water Lilies all year round.
