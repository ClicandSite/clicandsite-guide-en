Montparnasse, the Roaring Twenties quarter

In the 1920s, Picasso, Modigliani and Hemingway put the world to rights on the café terraces of Boulevard du Montparnasse. The cafés are still there, and the area hides free studio museums.

By Anthony, Cannes · Updated 12 November 2026

In the early 20th century, Montparnasse took over from Montmartre as the artists’ quarter. Painters, sculptors and writers from all over the world settled in its cheap studios and met at the Vavin crossroads, in the big brasseries of Boulevard du Montparnasse.

Today the area revolves around Montparnasse station and its tower, but the spirit of the 1920s lives on in the historic cafés, in small museums set in former studios and in the cemetery, where many artists are buried.

Things to see in Montparnasse

The Vavin crossroads cafés

La Rotonde, Le Dôme, Le Select and La Coupole, the four historic brasseries of Boulevard du Montparnasse.

Montparnasse Cemetery

Sartre and Beauvoir, Baudelaire, Serge Gainsbourg, Jacques Demy and Agnès Varda are buried here. Free entry.

The Musée Bourdelle

The studio and garden of sculptor Antoine Bourdelle, with his monumental bronzes. Permanent collections free.

The Musée Zadkine

Sculptor Ossip Zadkine’s little house and studio with its garden, near the Luxembourg. Permanent collections free.

The Catacombs

At Denfert-Rochereau, fifteen minutes’ walk away, the most famous underground ossuary in the world.

The Tour Montparnasse

The only skyscraper in central Paris; its observation deck has been closed for works since 31 March 2026.

How to get to Montparnasse

By metro

Vavin (line 4) for the cafés, Montparnasse-Bienvenüe (lines 4, 6, 12, 13) for the station, Edgar Quinet (line 6) for the cemetery.

On foot

The Luxembourg Gardens and the Latin Quarter are ten minutes away along Rue Vavin.

Practical info 2026: Montparnasse

  • Tour Montparnasse: the observation deck has been closed since 31 March 2026 for several years of works. For a view from above, climb the Arc de Triomphe or the Eiffel Tower.
  • Catacombs: online booking required, slots open seven days ahead; closed on Mondays.
  • Bourdelle and Zadkine museums: permanent collections free, closed on Mondays.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Montparnasse

  • Have a coffee on the terrace at Le Select or La Rotonde, then enter the cemetery from Boulevard Edgar-Quinet.
  • Ask for the cemetery map at the entrance to find the famous graves.
  • Book the Catacombs as soon as slots open: they often sell out.

Frequently asked questions

Can you still go up the Tour Montparnasse?

No, the observation deck has been closed since 31 March 2026 for several years of works.

Who is buried in Montparnasse Cemetery?

Among others, Jean-Paul Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir, Charles Baudelaire, Serge Gainsbourg, Jacques Demy and Agnès Varda.

Which free museums are in Montparnasse?

The Musée Bourdelle and the Musée Zadkine, free for their permanent collections.
