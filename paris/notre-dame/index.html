Visiting Notre-Dame de Paris

Reopened in December 2024 after the fire, the cathedral has its pale stone and its light back. Entry is free; booking a slot saves a lot of time.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Restored in five years after the April 2019 fire, Notre-Dame reopened in December 2024. The nave, cleaned of centuries of dust, is brighter than anyone alive remembers, and Viollet-le-Duc’s spire has been rebuilt exactly as it was.

Entry to the cathedral is free. A free booking system, opening two to three days ahead, lets you skip the queue; it is most useful from April to October. The towers and the archaeological crypt are paid for separately.

A bit of history

Construction of Notre-Dame began in 1163 under Bishop Maurice de Sully and took nearly two centuries. After damage during the Revolution, Victor Hugo’s 1831 novel revived interest in the cathedral, which was restored by Viollet-le-Duc, who added the spire.

On 15 April 2019 a fire destroyed the roof timbers and brought down the spire. After a five-year restoration, the cathedral reopened on 7 and 8 December 2024.

Things to do at Notre-Dame

The nave and choir

The new liturgical furniture, the rose windows and the restored side chapels. Take time to look up at the vaults. Book tickets or a guided tour at Notre-Dame

The rose windows

The three great medieval rose windows survived the fire. The south rose is brightest in late morning.

The towers

After several hundred steps, the gallery of chimeras and a view over the Île de la Cité. Separate ticket and booking.

The archaeological crypt

Under the square, the remains of ancient Lutetia and medieval Paris.

Square Jean-XXIII

Behind the cathedral, the best view of the apse and the flying buttresses.

How to get to Notre-Dame

By metro and RER

Cité (line 4) or Saint-Michel - Notre-Dame (RER B and C). The cathedral is a few minutes’ walk away.

On foot

From the Latin Quarter, the Marais or the Louvre, the bridges take you to the Île de la Cité in a quarter of an hour.

Practical info 2026: Notre-Dame

  • Cathedral: free entry. A free (optional) booking opens 2 to 3 days ahead on the official website and lets you skip the queue.
  • Opening hours: weekdays from about 7:45am to 7pm, until 10pm on Thursdays; weekends 8:15am to 7:30pm.
  • Towers: €16, sold online only, no entry without a booking (free for under-18s, slot still required). Treasury: €12. Archaeological crypt: €9.
  • Christmas market: square René-Viviani, facing the cathedral, 26 November to 27 December 2026.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Notre-Dame

  • Book your free slot as soon as it opens, two to three days ahead: they go fast.
  • Combine it with the Sainte-Chapelle, five minutes’ walk away on the same island.
  • Dress modestly; services go on during visiting hours, so keep your voice down.

Frequently asked questions

Is Notre-Dame free to visit?

Yes, entry to the cathedral is free. Only the towers and the archaeological crypt charge admission.

Do I need to book Notre-Dame?

It is not compulsory, but booking a free slot avoids a long wait, especially from April to October.

Can you climb Notre-Dame’s towers?

Yes, with a paid ticket and a booking. There are several hundred steps and no lift.

How long should you spend at Notre-Dame?

Allow 1 hour, 2 with the towers.

What is there to see near Notre-Dame?

In this guide, the closest places are Saint-Michel (about 5 min on foot), the Conciergerie (about 10 min on foot) and Sainte-Chapelle (about 10 min on foot).
