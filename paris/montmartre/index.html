Visiting Montmartre and the Sacré-Cœur

A village on a hill above Paris: steps, vines, painters’ studios and, at the top, the white basilica of the Sacré-Cœur.

Montmartre is the highest point in Paris. Once a village of windmills and vineyards, it drew Renoir, Picasso, Toulouse-Lautrec and Van Gogh at the end of the 19th century. Place du Tertre has become very touristy, but the streets on the far side of the hill are still quiet.

Entry to the Sacré-Cœur basilica is free. The dome costs extra: about 300 steps, no lift, for one of the best views of the city. The funicular saves you the steps from Square Louise-Michel for the price of a metro ticket.

Things to do in Montmartre

The Sacré-Cœur basilica

Free entry, with one of the largest mosaics in France above the choir. The forecourt looks out over the whole city.

The dome

About 300 spiral steps, then an open-air gallery around the cupola: the highest view in Paris after the Eiffel Tower.

Place du Tertre

Painters and portrait artists at work. Very busy in the afternoon; nicer early in the morning.

The Montmartre Museum

In the oldest house on the hill, with Suzanne Valadon’s studio and the Renoir gardens.

The vineyard and Rue de l’Abreuvoir

The Clos Montmartre, La Maison Rose and the cobbled streets on the north side, much quieter.

The Wall of Love

In Square Jehan-Rictus, Place des Abbesses: "I love you" written in hundreds of languages.

How to get to Montmartre

By metro

Anvers (line 2) for the funicular and the Sacré-Cœur, or Abbesses (line 12) to walk up through the village.

By funicular

It links the bottom of the hill to the Sacré-Cœur forecourt with an ordinary metro ticket.

On foot

From Abbesses, allow a quarter of an hour uphill via Rue des Abbesses and Rue Lepic.

Tips for visiting Montmartre

  • Walk up from Abbesses and come down the Sacré-Cœur steps: the climb is gentler and prettier.
  • At the foot of the steps, ignore anyone trying to tie a bracelet on your wrist, and watch your pockets in the crowd.
  • Sunset from the forecourt is magical, but everyone has the same idea: arrive early.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Sacré-Cœur free?

Yes. Only the climb to the dome is paid.

Is there a lift to the Sacré-Cœur dome?

No, you climb about 300 spiral steps.

How do you get to the Sacré-Cœur without the steps?

Take the funicular from Anvers metro station: it works with a metro ticket.
