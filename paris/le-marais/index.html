Visiting Le Marais

17th-century mansions, free museums, falafel and shops open on Sundays: the Marais is the most pleasant neighbourhood in Paris to explore on foot.

By Anthony, Cannes · Updated 12 November 2026

Once the aristocrats’ quarter, the Marais has kept its narrow streets and grand town houses, many of which are now museums. It stretches between the Hôtel de Ville, Place des Vosges and the Temple district.

It is also one of the few areas of Paris where most shops open on Sundays. Two major museums are free for their permanent collections: the Musée Carnavalet, on the history of Paris, and Victor Hugo’s House.

Things to do in the Marais

Place des Vosges

The oldest royal square in Paris, ringed by arcades. The garden in the middle is perfect for a break.

Victor Hugo’s House

The writer’s apartment on Place des Vosges. Permanent collections free.

The Musée Carnavalet

The history of Paris from the beginning to the present, in two mansions. Free for the permanent collections, closed on Mondays.

The Picasso Museum

The largest public collection of Picasso’s work, in the Hôtel Salé.

Rue des Rosiers

The heart of the historic Jewish quarter, famous for its falafel.

The Hôtel de Ville

The neo-Renaissance city hall and, in winter, a temporary ice rink on the square.

How to get to the Marais

By metro

Saint-Paul (line 1), Chemin Vert (line 8) for Place des Vosges, or Hôtel de Ville (lines 1 and 11).

On foot

From Notre-Dame, cross the Île Saint-Louis: a quarter of an hour to Rue des Rosiers.

Tips for visiting the Marais

  • On Sundays the area is lively and the shops are open; Rue des Rosiers is quieter on Saturday, the Sabbath.
  • The Musée Carnavalet is closed on Mondays; check before planning your walk around it.
  • Head to the Marché des Enfants-Rouges, the oldest covered market in Paris, for lunch.

Frequently asked questions

Are the Marais shops open on Sundays?

Yes, most shops in the area open on Sundays, which makes it an ideal Sunday outing.

Which free museums are in the Marais?

The Musée Carnavalet and Victor Hugo’s House, free for their permanent collections.

How do I get to the Marais?

By metro to Saint-Paul (line 1) or Chemin Vert (line 8), or on foot from Notre-Dame.
