Pigalle, the Paris of cabarets

The heart of Parisian nightlife at the foot of Montmartre: the Moulin Rouge, legendary concert halls and, just below, SoPi, one of the liveliest corners of the city.

Pigalle runs along the Boulevard de Clichy and Boulevard de Rochechouart, between Place Blanche and Anvers, at the foot of Montmartre. Long the district of cabarets and nightlife, it is still home to the Moulin Rouge and concert halls such as La Cigale and Le Trianon.

South of Place Pigalle, the area nicknamed SoPi (South Pigalle) has changed a lot: bakeries, cafés, shops and small restaurants have moved in around Rue des Martyrs, and two little-known museums are well worth a visit.

A bit of history

The district is named after the 18th-century sculptor Jean-Baptiste Pigalle. In the late 19th century, at the foot of Montmartre, it became the realm of cabarets and artists’ cafés: Le Chat Noir opened on Boulevard de Rochechouart in 1881 and the Moulin Rouge in 1889.

Long known for its seedy nightlife, lower Pigalle, nicknamed “SoPi”, has since the 2010s become an area of fashionable bars and restaurants.

Things to see in Pigalle

The Moulin Rouge

The most famous cabaret in the world, with its red windmill on Boulevard de Clichy. Admire the façade for free or book the Féerie revue. Book a guided tour or activity in Pigalle

Place Pigalle

A square with its fountain, the historic heart of the 19th-century artists’ quarter.

Rue des Martyrs

A sloping street lined with bakeries, cheese shops and pâtisseries, running from Notre-Dame-de-Lorette up to Montmartre.

The Musée de la Vie Romantique

The home of painter Ary Scheffer, with its cobbled courtyard, garden and tea room. Permanent collections free.

The Musée Gustave Moreau

The Symbolist painter’s house and studio, with its famous spiral staircase.

The concert halls

La Cigale, Le Trianon, La Machine du Moulin Rouge: the area is still one of the great live music spots in Paris.

How to get to Pigalle

By metro

Pigalle (lines 2 and 12), Blanche (line 2) for the Moulin Rouge and Saint-Georges (line 12) for SoPi.

On foot

Rue des Martyrs or Rue Lepic climb to Montmartre and the Sacré-Cœur in a quarter of an hour.

Practical info 2026: Pigalle

  • Musée de la Vie Romantique (16 rue Chaptal): permanent collections free, closed on Mondays.

Tips for visiting Pigalle

  • Boulevard de Clichy still has sex shops and touts: keep walking if someone pushes an offer outside a venue.
  • Walk up Rue des Martyrs in late morning when the shops are open, then carry on up to Montmartre.
  • Keep the evening for a concert or a show at the Moulin Rouge.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pigalle safe?

Yes, it is a busy area day and night. Keep an eye on your belongings and ignore the touts on the boulevard.

What can you do in Pigalle during the day?

Walk up Rue des Martyrs, visit the Musée de la Vie Romantique and the Musée Gustave Moreau, then climb to Montmartre.

How do I get to the Moulin Rouge?

By metro to Blanche (line 2), right opposite the cabaret.

How long should you spend in Pigalle?

Allow half a day, or an evening.

What is there to see near Pigalle?

In this guide, the closest places are Moulin Rouge (about 10 min on foot), Montmartre (about 10 min on foot) and Sacré-Cœur (about 10 min on foot).
