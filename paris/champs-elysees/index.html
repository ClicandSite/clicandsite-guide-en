The Champs-Élysées, from Concorde to the Arc de Triomphe

Two kilometres of grand perspective, hundreds of trees lit up in winter and the Arc de Triomphe at the end. The world’s most famous avenue is best explored on foot.

The Avenue des Champs-Élysées runs from the Place de la Concorde to the Place Charles-de-Gaulle, home of the Arc de Triomphe. The lower end is lined with gardens, the upper end with shops, cinemas and cafés.

In winter the avenue lights up: for 2026 the switch-on is announced for Sunday 22 November, and the lights stay on until early January 2027. This year also sees a Christmas market return to the Champs-Élysées after about ten years away.

A bit of history

In 1667 Louis XIV’s gardener André Le Nôtre laid out a broad tree-lined walk extending the Tuileries garden. It took the name Champs-Élysées in the early 18th century, after the Elysian Fields where heroes rested in Greek myth.

In the 19th century it became the smart promenade of Paris. It hosts the Bastille Day military parade and, since 1975, the finish of the Tour de France.

Things to do on the Champs-Élysées

The Christmas lights

Every evening the trees along the avenue glow with lights, from Concorde to the Étoile. The view is best walking down from the Arc de Triomphe. Book a guided tour or activity on the Champs-Élysées

The Arc de Triomphe

At the top of the avenue, its terrace gives the best view of the illuminated Champs-Élysées.

The Grand Palais and Petit Palais

Right by the lower end of the avenue; the Petit Palais, free for its permanent collections, is ideal on a cold day.

Place de la Concorde

The obelisk and a view of the Eiffel Tower, where the walk begins.

The Christmas market

Chalets and treats along the avenue during the 2026 festive season.

How to get to the Champs-Élysées

By metro

Line 1, which runs the length of the avenue: Concorde, Champs-Élysées-Clemenceau, Franklin-D.-Roosevelt, George V and Charles-de-Gaulle-Étoile.

By RER

RER A, Charles-de-Gaulle-Étoile station, at the foot of the Arc de Triomphe.

On foot

Allow about 30 to 45 minutes to stroll the full length of the avenue.

Practical info 2026: The Champs-Élysées

  • Christmas lights: switched on Sunday 22 November 2026, then every evening until 3 January 2027.
  • Christmas market: back on the avenue after about ten years away, from 22 November 2026, between place de la Concorde and the Rond-Point des Champs-Élysées.
  • Arc de Triomphe: €16 online in low season, reached only through the underpasses.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for the Champs-Élysées

  • Come at dusk, around 5pm in winter, to see the lights come on.
  • Climb the Arc de Triomphe in the evening to see the whole avenue lit up.
  • Keep an eye on your belongings: the avenue is very busy, especially over the holidays.
