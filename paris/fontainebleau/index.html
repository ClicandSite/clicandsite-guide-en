The Château de Fontainebleau, an hour from Paris

Home to French kings for seven centuries and then to Napoleon, Fontainebleau is quieter than Versailles and just as splendid.

By Anthony, Cannes · Updated 12 November 2026

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Château de Fontainebleau was home to rulers from the Middle Ages to Napoleon III. François I made it a centre of the Renaissance in France, and Napoleon called it “the true home of kings”.

The château sits in the heart of the Fontainebleau forest, about 60 km southeast of Paris. It makes an easy day trip by train and a great way to escape the Versailles crowds.

What to see at Fontainebleau

The François I Gallery

Italian Renaissance frescoes and woodwork, one of the château’s most precious ensembles.

Napoleon’s apartments

The throne room and the Emperor’s apartments, furnished as he knew them.

The Courtyard of Farewells

The main courtyard and its horseshoe staircase, where Napoleon bade farewell to his guard in 1814.

The gardens and park

The formal parterre, the English garden and the grand canal, free to enter.

How to get to Fontainebleau from Paris

By train

From Gare de Lyon to Fontainebleau-Avon, then bus line 1 to the château.

By car

About an hour on the A6, depending on traffic.

On a day trip

Day tours from Paris often combine Fontainebleau with Vaux-le-Vicomte.

Practical info 2026: Fontainebleau

  • Palace: €17 full price, €15 reduced; free for under-18s and EU citizens aged 18-25, and for everyone on the first Sunday of the month (except July and August).
  • Opening hours: October to March, 9:30am–5pm (last entry 45 minutes before); closed on Tuesdays, 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. The courtyards and gardens are free, even on Tuesdays.
  • From Paris: Transilien line R from Gare de Lyon to Fontainebleau-Avon, then bus 1 to the palace.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Fontainebleau

  • The château is closed on Tuesdays: check before you set off.
  • Until 25 January 2027, an exhibition is devoted to Marie Antoinette.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for the gardens and perhaps a walk in the forest.
  • Entry is free for under-18s.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Château de Fontainebleau cost?

A full-price ticket costs €17 in 2026 (€15 reduced). It is free for under-18s and the gardens are free to enter.

What are the opening hours?

From October to March it is open from 9.30am to 5pm, except on Tuesdays. It is closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

How do I get to Fontainebleau without a car?

Take the train from Gare de Lyon to Fontainebleau-Avon, then bus line 1, which stops outside the château.
