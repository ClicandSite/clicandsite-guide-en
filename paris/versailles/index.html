Visiting the Palace of Versailles

The Hall of Mirrors, the King’s apartments, Le Nôtre’s gardens and Marie-Antoinette’s estate: a full day out, forty minutes from Paris.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Louis XIV turned his father’s hunting lodge into a royal residence and the seat of power. Today the estate covers the palace, the formal gardens, the Grand and Petit Trianon and the Queen’s Hamlet, over hundreds of hectares.

The palace is closed on Mondays. Booking a time slot is compulsory for everyone, even free entry. The Passport ticket covers the palace and the Trianon estate; the gardens are free except on Musical Fountains days in the warmer months.

A bit of history

Versailles was at first just a hunting lodge built by Louis XIII in 1623. Louis XIV turned it into a vast palace with Le Vau, Le Brun, Le Nôtre and Hardouin-Mansart, then moved the court and government there in 1682.

The Hall of Mirrors was finished in 1684, and the Treaty of Versailles was signed there on 28 June 1919. The palace became a museum under Louis-Philippe in 1837 and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979.

Things to do at Versailles

The Hall of Mirrors

357 mirrors and ceilings painted by Le Brun. Go first thing or late in the day to see it without the crowds. Book tickets or a guided tour at Versailles

The State Apartments

The King’s and Queen’s apartments, with Louis XIV’s bedchamber at the centre of the palace.

The gardens

The Grand Canal, the fountains and Le Nôtre’s groves. You can hire a rowing boat or a bike in the warmer months.

The Grand and Petit Trianon

The royal family’s more private residences, half an hour’s walk from the palace. They open at noon.

The Queen’s Hamlet

Marie-Antoinette’s country village, with a farm, a mill and a pond.

The Gallery of Coaches

In the Great Stables opposite the palace: the coronation coaches, free to visit.

How to get to Versailles

By RER C

To Versailles Château Rive Gauche, the closest station, ten minutes’ walk away. Allow about 40 minutes from central Paris.

By Transilien train

Line L from Saint-Lazare to Versailles Rive Droite, or line N from Montparnasse to Versailles Chantiers. About twenty minutes’ walk from there.

By car or with a driver

Paid parking on Place d’Armes. A driver saves you connections, especially with children.

Practical info 2026: Versailles

  • Passport ticket (palace, Trianon, gardens): in low season €22 for EEA visitors and €25 for everyone else; in high season €32 and €35.
  • Free for under-18s and EU residents under 26; free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month from November to March.
  • Closed on Mondays and on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. A timed slot is required for the palace.
  • Hours from November to March: palace 9 am to 5.30 pm (last admission 5 pm), Trianon and Marie-Antoinette’s estate noon to 5.30 pm, gardens 8 am to 6 pm. From April to October, the palace and Trianon close at 6.30 pm.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Versailles

  • Avoid Tuesday and Sunday: they are the busiest days, since the palace is closed on Monday.
  • Start with the gardens and Trianon if your palace slot is in the early afternoon.
  • In winter the palace is free on the first Sunday of the month, but you still have to book a slot.

Frequently asked questions

What day is Versailles closed?

Mondays, plus 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

Do I need to book Versailles?

Yes, a time slot is compulsory for every visitor, including those who get in free.

How do I get to Versailles from Paris?

The easiest way is RER C to Versailles Château Rive Gauche, ten minutes’ walk from the palace.

How long should you spend at Versailles?

Allow a full day.

What is there to see near Versailles?

In this guide, the closest places are La Défense (about 16 km away), Louis Vuitton Foundation (about 16 km away) and Trocadéro (about 17 km away).
