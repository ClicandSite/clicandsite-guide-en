Visiting the Louvre

You cannot see the world’s largest museum in one go: you plan it. Book your time slot, pick three or four sections and avoid Tuesday, when it is closed.

Once the palace of the kings of France, the Louvre holds collections from Antiquity to the 19th century across three wings: Denon, Sully and Richelieu. The Mona Lisa, the Venus de Milo and the Winged Victory of Samothrace are the stars, but they are a tiny part of what there is to see.

Booking a time slot is compulsory, including for visitors who get in free. The museum is closed on Tuesdays and stays open late on Wednesday and Friday evenings, which are often quieter.

Things to do at the Louvre

The Mona Lisa

In the Salle des États, Denon wing. The queue forms in front of the painting: go there first at opening time, or at the end of a late opening.

The Winged Victory of Samothrace

At the top of the Daru staircase, one of the most dramatic displays in the museum.

The Venus de Milo

In the Greek galleries of the Sully wing, on the ground floor.

The great French paintings

The Raft of the Medusa, Liberty Leading the People, the Coronation of Napoleon: the large-format gallery in the Denon wing, right next to the Mona Lisa.

Napoleon III’s apartments

Gilding, chandeliers and state rooms in the Richelieu wing, usually less crowded.

The Pyramid and Cour Napoléon

I. M. Pei’s glass pyramid, worth seeing from outside by day and by night.

How to get to the Louvre

By metro

Palais-Royal - Musée du Louvre (lines 1 and 7). Louvre - Rivoli on line 1 is a little further.

Which entrance?

The main entrance is under the Pyramid. The Carrousel entrance, at 99 Rue de Rivoli, is indoors and handy when it rains. Follow the instructions on your ticket.

On foot

The museum is a few minutes from the Pont des Arts, the Tuileries and the Île de la Cité.

Tips for visiting the Louvre

  • Plan your route before you go in: three well-prepared hours beat a day of wandering.
  • Save the Mona Lisa for opening time or the end of a late opening; the rest of the museum is far calmer.
  • There are cafés and restaurants inside: take a proper break to keep going.

Frequently asked questions

What day is the Louvre closed?

Tuesdays, plus a few public holidays. It stays open late on Wednesday and Friday.

Do I need to book the Louvre?

Yes, a time slot is compulsory for every visitor, even those who get in free.

How long do you need at the Louvre?

Allow three hours for the main masterpieces. A whole day is not enough to see everything: better to come back.
