Visiting the Paris Catacombs

Twenty metres below Paris, the bones of millions of Parisians line the galleries. Renovated in 2026, the Catacombs can only be visited with a booking.

At the end of the 18th century, Paris emptied its overflowing cemeteries and moved the bones into old underground quarries. The ossuary, open to the public since the early 19th century, has become one of the most striking visits in the city.

After several months of renovation, the Catacombs reopened on 8 April 2026 with new lighting and an updated visit. Booking a time slot online is compulsory: you cannot get in without a reserved ticket.

Things to do in the Catacombs

The ossuary

Kilometres of skulls and bones stacked with care, sometimes in patterns, along galleries marked with inscriptions.

The old quarries

Before the ossuary, the route passes through the limestone quarries that supplied some of the stone of Paris.

The renovated route

New lighting, models and an audio guide that explain the history of the city’s underground.

How to get to the Catacombs

By metro and RER

Denfert-Rochereau station (lines 4 and 6, RER B), right by the entrance on Avenue du Colonel-Henri-Rol-Tanguy.

The exit

You come out elsewhere, on Rue Rémy-Dumoncel, a few minutes’ walk away: bear it in mind if you are meeting someone.

Tips for visiting the Catacombs

  • Bring a jacket: it is about 14 °C underground all year round.
  • Wear closed shoes; the ground is uneven and sometimes damp.
  • The visit is not recommended for claustrophobic visitors or people with reduced mobility, and is not ideal for young children.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book the Paris Catacombs?

Yes, booking a time slot online is compulsory, and slots go fast, especially at weekends and during holidays.

What day are the Catacombs closed?

Mondays, plus 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

How many steps are there in the Catacombs?

131 steps down at the start and 112 back up at the exit, with no lift.
