Belleville, working-class and cosmopolitan Paris

A former village of vineyards turned home to artists and cuisines from around the world, with one of the best views of Paris from the top of its park.

Belleville was a village of vineyards and open-air dance halls, absorbed into Paris in 1860. Today it straddles the 10th, 11th, 19th and 20th arrondissements, on a hill climbing up from Boulevard de Belleville.

It is a lively working-class area where one of the city’s Chinatowns sits alongside restaurants from all over the world, artists’ studios and walls covered in graffiti. You come not for monuments but for the atmosphere and the view.

A bit of history

A hilltop village of vineyards and open-air taverns, Belleville was annexed by Paris in 1860. As a working-class district, it was one of the last strongholds of the Paris Commune in May 1871.

In the 20th century it welcomed wave after wave of immigrants: Eastern European Jews, Armenians, North Africans, then Chinese. Legend has it that Édith Piaf was born on the steps of 72 rue de Belleville in 1915.

Things to see in Belleville

Parc de Belleville

A terraced hillside park with a viewpoint looking out over the whole of Paris, Eiffel Tower included. Book a guided tour or activity in Belleville

Rue Dénoyez

A small street known for its painted walls and graffiti, which change all the time.

Boulevard and Rue de Belleville

The heart of Belleville’s Chinatown: restaurants, grocers and Asian bakeries.

72 Rue de Belleville

A plaque recalls the legend that Édith Piaf was born on the steps of this building.

Parc des Buttes-Chaumont

Ten minutes’ walk away, a large romantic park with a lake, cliffs and the Temple de la Sibylle.

How to get to Belleville

By metro

Belleville (lines 2 and 11) for the lower part, Pyrénées (line 11) for the top of the park.

On foot

The Canal Saint-Martin is a quarter of an hour away, Père-Lachaise twenty minutes down Rue de Ménilmontant.

Practical info 2026: Belleville

  • Parc de Belleville: free, during the opening hours of Paris city gardens.
  • Artists’ studios: the Belleville studios open to the public on open-studio days; check the dates on the association’s website.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Belleville

  • Take the metro up to Pyrénées and walk down through the park: the view is in front of you, no climbing needed.
  • Come at sunset for the view from the park’s viewpoint.
  • For lunch, the Chinese and Vietnamese restaurants on Boulevard de Belleville are among the cheapest in Paris.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best view in Belleville?

From the viewpoint at the top of Parc de Belleville, on Rue Piat, overlooking the whole city.

Is Belleville worth visiting?

Yes, especially for wandering, eating and street art; it is a lively, working-class area off the tourist trail.

How do I get to Belleville?

By metro to Belleville (lines 2 and 11) or Pyrénées (line 11).

How long should you spend in Belleville?

Allow two to three hours.

What is there to see near Belleville?

In this guide, the closest places are Parc des Buttes-Chaumont (about 15 min on foot), Père Lachaise Cemetery (about 25 min on foot) and Canal Saint-Martin (about 25 min on foot).
