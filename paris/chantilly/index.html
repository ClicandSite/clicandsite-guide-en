The Château de Chantilly, 25 minutes from Paris

A château surrounded by water, one of France’s finest collections of old master paintings and princely stables, half an hour by train from Paris.

Less famous than Versailles, the Chantilly estate is one of the best day trips from Paris. The château was left to the Institut de France by the Duke of Aumale, son of the last King of the French, together with his collections.

The Condé Museum holds France’s second-largest collection of old master paintings after the Louvre. Around it, the Great Stables, the Horse Museum and a vast park partly designed by Le Nôtre make for a full day.

A bit of history

For centuries Chantilly belonged to two great families, the Montmorencys and then the Princes of Condé. The Great Stables, built in the 18th century for the Prince of Condé, could house 240 horses.

The main château, destroyed during the Revolution, was rebuilt in the 19th century by the Duke of Aumale, son of King Louis-Philippe. A great collector, he left the estate and its collections to the Institut de France on condition that they never be changed: this is the Musée Condé.

What to see at Chantilly

The Condé Museum

Raphael, Poussin, Ingres and hundreds of paintings hung as they were in the 19th century. Book tickets or a guided tour at Chantilly

The Great Stables

Monumental 18th-century stables that house the Horse Museum.

The park

French formal gardens, an English garden, a hamlet and the grand canal.

Chantilly cream

Taste it on site, where the recipe got its name.

How to get to Chantilly from Paris

By train

TER from Gare du Nord to Chantilly-Gouvieux in about 25 minutes, or RER D in about 45 minutes.

From the station

A free local shuttle, or a 25-minute walk through town.

By car

About 45 minutes on the A1; paid parking on site, €5 a day.

Practical info 2026: Chantilly

  • Estate ticket (château and Musée Condé, park, Great Stables, Horse Museum): €18, €14.50 reduced; free under 7.
  • Winter hours (November to March): château 10am–5pm, Great Stables noon–5pm, park 10am–6pm; closed on Tuesdays, with an annual closure in January.
  • Getting there: TER from Paris-Gare du Nord to Chantilly-Gouvieux; by car, parking costs €5.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Chantilly

  • The estate is closed on Tuesdays.
  • The Pack TER from Gare du Nord combines the train and entry: handy for a car-free day.
  • Arrive at opening time to enjoy the Condé Museum in peace.
  • Allow a full day for the château, the stables and the park.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Château de Chantilly cost?

The estate ticket costs €18 in 2026. It covers the château, the park, the Great Stables and the Horse Museum.

How do I get to Chantilly by train?

Take a TER from Gare du Nord to Chantilly-Gouvieux (about 25 minutes), then the shuttle or a 25-minute walk to the château.

What are the opening hours?

In low season the château is open from 10.30am to 5pm, except Tuesdays. In high season it opens from 10am to 6pm.

How long should you spend at Chantilly?

Allow a day from Paris.

What is there to see near Chantilly?

In this guide, the closest places are Auvers-sur-Oise (about 33 km away), La Villette (about 43 km away) and Parc des Buttes-Chaumont (about 45 km away).
