La Villette, science and music in a giant park

A huge park in north-east Paris with Europe’s largest science museum, a spectacular concert hall and gardens to explore with the family.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Parc de la Villette, in the 19th arrondissement, stands on the site of Paris’s former slaughterhouses and cattle market. In the 1980s it was turned into a large urban park dotted with red pavilions, the “folies”, and crossed by the Canal de l’Ourcq.

Today it is home to several major cultural venues: the Cité des Sciences et de l’Industrie, the Géode, the Grande Halle, the Zénith, the Cité de la Musique and the Philharmonie de Paris. An ideal family day out, especially in bad weather.

A bit of history

For over a century La Villette housed the Paris slaughterhouses and cattle market, opened under Napoleon III in 1867 and closed in 1974. The iron-and-glass Grande Halle is the former cattle hall.

In the 1980s the site became a cultural park designed by Bernard Tschumi, dotted with red “follies”. La Géode opened in 1985, the Cité des Sciences in 1986 and Jean Nouvel’s Philharmonie in 2015.

Things to see at La Villette

The Cité des Sciences et de l’Industrie

Europe’s largest science museum, with interactive exhibitions, a planetarium and the Cité des Enfants. Closed on Mondays. Book tickets or a guided tour at La Villette

The Géode

The giant mirrored sphere in front of the Cité des Sciences, housing a cinema with a hemispherical screen.

The Argonaute

A real 1950s submarine you can board at the foot of the Cité des Sciences.

The Philharmonie de Paris

Jean Nouvel’s concert hall, opened in 2015, and the Musée de la Musique in the neighbouring Cité de la Musique.

The Grande Halle

The 19th-century iron-and-glass former cattle hall, now used for exhibitions and fairs.

The park and its gardens

Themed gardens, big lawns and the banks of the Canal de l’Ourcq, for a break between visits.

How to get to La Villette

By metro and tram

Porte de la Villette (line 7) for the Cité des Sciences, Porte de Pantin (line 5) for the Philharmonie; tram T3b stops at both.

On foot

From the Canal Saint-Martin, follow the Bassin de la Villette and then the Canal de l’Ourcq: about forty minutes.

Practical info 2026: La Villette

  • Cité des Sciences: closed on Mondays, 1 January, 1 May and 25 December; the Cité des Enfants needs a separate ticket.
  • Philharmonie rooftop: open from mid-March to early November, so closed in winter.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting La Villette

  • Come on a weekday outside school holidays: the Cité des Sciences gets very busy at weekends.
  • Book a planetarium slot as soon as you arrive, as shows fill up fast.
  • In the evening, carry on with a concert at the Philharmonie or a show at the Zénith.

Frequently asked questions

Which day is the Cité des Sciences closed?

Mondays, as well as 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

Does the Cité des Sciences ticket include the Cité des Enfants?

No, the Cité des Enfants needs a separate ticket and a booked time slot.

How do I get to Parc de la Villette?

By metro to Porte de la Villette (line 7) or Porte de Pantin (line 5), or by tram T3b.

How long should you spend at La Villette?

Allow a full day.

What is there to see near La Villette?

In this guide, the closest places are Parc des Buttes-Chaumont (about 25 min on foot), Belleville (about 3 km away) and Canal Saint-Martin (about 4 km away).
