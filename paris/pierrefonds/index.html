Pierrefonds and Compiègne, the Emperor’s castles

A fairy-tale castle rebuilt for Napoleon III, and the palace where the imperial court spent its autumns, in the heart of a vast forest.

About 80 kilometres north of Paris, the Forest of Compiègne is home to two great castles linked to Napoleon III. The Palais de Compiègne, a royal and then imperial residence, welcomed the court every autumn for its famous “séries” of parties and hunts.

Some fifteen kilometres away, the Château de Pierrefonds was a medieval ruin when the Emperor asked Viollet-le-Duc to rebuild it. The result is an idealised fortress with towers, machicolations and decorated halls, which has appeared in many films.

A bit of history

Pierrefonds castle was built in the late 14th century for Louis of Orléans, brother of King Charles VI. Dismantled in the 17th century on Louis XIII’s orders, it lay in ruins for two hundred years.

Napoleon I bought the ruins, and Napoleon III, who often stayed at the nearby palace of Compiègne, had Viollet-le-Duc rebuild it from 1857: today’s castle is his vision of an ideal medieval fortress.

Things to see in Pierrefonds and Compiègne

The Château de Pierrefonds

Viollet-le-Duc’s dream castle, with its courtyard, Salle des Preux and wall walks. Book tickets or a guided tour in Pierrefonds and Compiègne

The Palais de Compiègne

The Emperor’s and Empress’s apartments, the ballroom and the Second Empire museum.

The palace park

A large park, free to enter, extended by an avenue running straight into the forest.

The Armistice Clearing

Where the armistice of 11 November 1918 was signed, with its memorial and a replica of the railway carriage.

The Forest of Compiègne

One of the largest forests in France, criss-crossed by forest roads, cycle paths and ponds.

How to get to Pierrefonds

By train

From Paris-Nord to Compiègne in about 40 minutes; the palace is ten minutes’ walk from the station.

By car

Via the A1, about an hour and a quarter to Compiègne; a car is almost essential for Pierrefonds, which has poor bus links.

Practical info 2026: Pierrefonds and Compiègne

  • Palais de Compiègne: open daily except Tuesday, 10am to 6pm; free for under-26s and on the first Sunday of the month.
  • Château de Pierrefonds: from early September to late April, open 10am to 5.30pm; free for under-18s.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Pierrefonds and Compiègne

  • Do Compiègne in the morning by train, then reach Pierrefonds by car or by bike through the forest in the afternoon.
  • On 11 November the Armistice Clearing hosts ceremonies: come another day if you want peace and quiet.
  • Combine with the Château de Chantilly, half an hour’s drive away.

Frequently asked questions

How can I get to Pierrefonds without a car?

By train to Compiègne, then bus or taxi; buses are infrequent, so a car or bike is more practical.

Which day is the Palais de Compiègne closed?

Tuesday; it is open the other days from 10am to 6pm.

Who rebuilt the Château de Pierrefonds?

The architect Eugène Viollet-le-Duc, at Napoleon III’s request, in the 19th century.

How long should you spend in Pierrefonds and Compiègne?

Allow a full day.

What is there to see near Pierrefonds and Compiègne?

In this guide, the closest places are Château de Chantilly (about 50 km away), Disneyland Paris (about 69 km away) and Auvers-sur-Oise (about 83 km away).
