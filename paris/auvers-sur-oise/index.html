Auvers-sur-Oise, Van Gogh’s village

Vincent van Gogh spent only seventy days here, but painted more than seventy canvases. The village has stayed almost as he knew it.

Auvers-sur-Oise is a village in the Val-d’Oise, on the River Oise, about thirty kilometres north of Paris. Daubigny, Pissarro and Cézanne painted here before Vincent van Gogh arrived in May 1890. He lodged at the Auberge Ravoux and painted feverishly for two months, until his death on 29 July 1890.

Today you can follow in his footsteps around the village: the church he painted, the wheat fields above the village, the cemetery where he lies with his brother Theo, and his tiny room at the inn, preserved almost intact.

A bit of history

In the 19th century the light along the Oise drew Daubigny, Pissarro and Cézanne. Vincent van Gogh arrived on 20 May 1890 and lodged at the Auberge Ravoux: in barely seventy days he painted around eighty canvases, including the church at Auvers and the wheat fields.

Wounded by a gunshot, he died at the inn on 29 July 1890. He is buried in the village cemetery beside his brother Theo.

Things to see in Auvers-sur-Oise

The Auberge Ravoux, Van Gogh’s house

The inn where the painter spent his last days, with his attic room no. 5 and its period dining room. Book tickets or a guided tour in Auvers-sur-Oise

Notre-Dame d’Auvers church

The Gothic church immortalised by Van Gogh, at the top of a flight of stone steps.

The grave of Vincent and Theo

The two brothers lie side by side under the ivy in the small cemetery above the fields. Free entry.

The Château d’Auvers

A 17th-century château with terraced gardens and an exhibition on the Impressionists.

Dr Gachet’s house

The home of the doctor and friend of painters who treated Van Gogh and sat for him.

How to get to Auvers-sur-Oise

By train

Transilien trains from Paris-Nord or Paris Saint-Lazare, changing at Pontoise or Saint-Ouen-l’Aumône depending on the timetable: about one hour.

By car

About 45 minutes from Paris via the A15 or A115, traffic permitting.

Practical info 2026: Auvers-sur-Oise

  • Auberge Ravoux (Van Gogh’s house): open from early March to around late November, Wednesday to Sunday; closed in winter.
  • Cemetery and church: free access all year, worth it even out of season.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Auvers-sur-Oise

  • Come between March and November to see Van Gogh’s room; in winter, the village and the grave are still accessible.
  • Walk up from the cemetery into the fields: that is where Van Gogh painted his last wheat-field landscapes.
  • Prepare your visit by seeing the Auvers canvases at the Musée d’Orsay, including The Church at Auvers.

Frequently asked questions

How long did Van Gogh live in Auvers-sur-Oise?

Seventy days, from May to July 1890, during which he painted more than seventy canvases.

Where is Van Gogh buried?

In the cemetery at Auvers-sur-Oise, next to his brother Theo; free access.

How do I get to Auvers-sur-Oise from Paris?

By Transilien train from Paris-Nord or Saint-Lazare, with one change, in about an hour.

How long should you spend in Auvers-sur-Oise?

Allow half a day.
