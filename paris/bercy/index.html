Bercy, from wine warehouses to modern Paris

Once Europe’s largest wine market, Bercy is now an area of parks, cinema and riverside walks, ideal for a breather away from the crowds.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Until the 1960s, Bercy was home to the warehouses and cellars of Europe’s largest wine market, along the Seine. The area was completely redeveloped from the 1980s: today it has the Ministry of Finance, the Accor Arena, a large park and new buildings.

A few traces of its wine-trade past remain, such as the cobbled rails in the park and the old wine warehouses of Cour Saint-Émilion, now shops and restaurants. Across the Seine, the Simone-de-Beauvoir footbridge leads to the François-Mitterrand National Library.

A bit of history

Long outside the city limits, Bercy became Europe’s largest wine market in the 19th century: hundreds of cellars and warehouses received barrels shipped along the Seine, beyond the reach of Paris’s entry tax.

The district was transformed from the 1980s with the indoor arena (1984), the Ministry of the Economy (1989) and then the Parc de Bercy. Cour Saint-Émilion kept some of the old wine stores, now shops and restaurants.

Things to see in Bercy

Parc de Bercy

A large park in three parts, with vegetable plots, vines, a romantic garden and old cobbled rails. Book a guided tour or activity in Bercy

Cour Saint-Émilion

The former wine warehouses, now a pedestrian street of shops, restaurants and terraces (Bercy Village).

The Cinémathèque Française

In a building by Frank Gehry, a film museum, exhibitions and screenings every day.

The Musée des Arts Forains

Vintage carousels, fairground organs and rides in old wine warehouses; visits by reservation only.

The Simone-de-Beauvoir footbridge

A wave-shaped footbridge across the Seine to the National Library.

How to get to Bercy

By metro

Bercy (lines 6 and 14) or Cour Saint-Émilion (line 14), a few minutes from Châtelet on line 14.

On foot

From the Bastille, follow the Arsenal marina and then the quays: about twenty-five minutes.

Practical info 2026: Bercy

  • Musée des Arts Forains (53 Avenue des Terroirs-de-France): guided tours only, by reservation, on certain dates.
  • Cinémathèque Française (51 Rue de Bercy): closed on Tuesdays; screening schedule on its website.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Bercy

  • Come on a Sunday: a walk in the park, lunch on Cour Saint-Émilion, then a film at the Cinémathèque.
  • Cross the Simone-de-Beauvoir footbridge at sunset for the view along the Seine.
  • Book the Musée des Arts Forains early: tours are few and sell out fast, especially over the holidays.

Frequently asked questions

What is there to do in Bercy?

Walk in Parc de Bercy, stroll along Cour Saint-Émilion, visit the Cinémathèque Française and cross the Simone-de-Beauvoir footbridge.

Can you visit the Musée des Arts Forains on your own?

No, only on guided tours booked in advance, on certain dates.

How do I get to Bercy?

By metro, line 14 (Bercy or Cour Saint-Émilion) or line 6 (Bercy).

How long should you spend in Bercy?

Allow half a day.

What is there to see near Bercy?

In this guide, the closest places are Bastille (about 3 km away), Le Marais (about 3 km away) and Île Saint-Louis (about 3 km away).
