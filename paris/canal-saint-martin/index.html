The Canal Saint-Martin, Paris by the water

Iron footbridges, locks, plane trees and café terraces: the Canal Saint-Martin is the Parisians’ favourite walk, far from the big monuments.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

The Canal Saint-Martin runs for 4.5 kilometres from the Bassin de la Villette to the Seine at the Arsenal marina. Dug in the early 19th century to bring water into Paris, it goes underground between Place de la Bastille and Rue du Faubourg-du-Temple.

The open-air stretch in the 10th arrondissement is the prettiest: nine locks, arched footbridges and tree-lined quays where people picnic as soon as the sun comes out.

Things to see along the Canal Saint-Martin

The locks and footbridges

Watch the barges go through the locks, then cross the green iron footbridges that arch over the canal. Book a guided tour or activity along the Canal Saint-Martin

The Hôtel du Nord

The façade from Marcel Carné’s film, on Quai de Jemmapes, now a restaurant.

Quai de Valmy and Quai de Jemmapes

The two banks of the canal, with their cafés, shops and waterside benches.

The Bassin de la Villette

Further north, the largest artificial lake in Paris, with cinemas, bars and water sports.

A canal cruise

A two-and-a-half-hour cruise from the Arsenal marina, through the locks and the underground vault beneath the Bastille.

How to get to the Canal Saint-Martin

By metro

République (lines 3, 5, 8, 9, 11), Jacques Bonsergent (line 5) or Gare de l’Est for the northern stretch.

On foot

From Le Marais, walk up to Place de la République: a quarter of an hour to the first locks.

Practical info 2026: Canal Saint-Martin

  • Canal cruises: departing from the Arsenal marina (near the Bastille) or the Bassin de la Villette, about two and a half hours; fewer sailings in winter, booking recommended.
  • Sundays: much of the quays are car-free in the afternoon, ideal for a stroll.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Canal Saint-Martin

  • Come on a Sunday afternoon, when the quays are given over to pedestrians and bikes.
  • For a proper waterside picnic, pick up lunch from the bakeries on Rue de Lancry or Rue Beaurepaire.
  • Combine with Père-Lachaise or Le Marais, both less than half an hour’s walk away.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the Canal Saint-Martin start?

It starts at the Bassin de la Villette in the north and meets the Seine at the Arsenal marina, near the Bastille.

Can you take a cruise on the Canal Saint-Martin?

Yes, cruises of about two and a half hours leave from the Arsenal marina or the Bassin de la Villette and pass through the locks.

When is the best time to visit the canal?

On a Sunday afternoon, when the quays are car-free, or in the early evening for the terrace atmosphere.

How long should you spend along the Canal Saint-Martin?

Allow two hours on foot.

What is there to see near the Canal Saint-Martin?

In this guide, the closest places are Picasso Museum (about 20 min on foot), Belleville (about 25 min on foot) and Châtelet-Les Halles (about 30 min on foot).
