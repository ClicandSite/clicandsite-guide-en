The Château de Vincennes, fortress of kings

At the end of metro line 1, a royal castle with Europe’s tallest medieval keep, a Gothic Sainte-Chapelle and, right next door, the vast Bois de Vincennes.

The Château de Vincennes was home to the kings of France for centuries, before they moved to Versailles. Its keep, built in the 14th century during the Hundred Years’ War, stands 52 metres tall: the tallest surviving medieval keep in Europe.

The enclosure also contains a Gothic Sainte-Chapelle with fine stained glass and 17th-century pavilions. The castle later served as a prison, holding Diderot, Mirabeau and the Marquis de Sade.

A bit of history

First a simple hunting lodge of the Capetian kings, Vincennes became a true fortress in the 14th century. The 52-metre keep, completed under Charles V, is the tallest in Europe; the king kept his library and treasury there.

The château later served as a state prison (Diderot and Mirabeau were held there). The Duke of Enghien was shot there in 1804 on Bonaparte’s orders. Today it houses the Defence Historical Service.

Things to see at Vincennes

The ramparts and moat

The fortified walls and towers that give the castle its fortress look.

The Bois de Vincennes

Right next door, the largest green space in Paris, with Lac Daumesnil, the Parc Floral and the zoo.

How to get to the Château de Vincennes

By metro and RER

Château de Vincennes, the terminus of line 1, four minutes’ walk from the entrance; or RER A to Vincennes.

From central Paris

Line 1 runs directly from Châtelet, the Louvre and the Bastille.

Practical info 2026: Château de Vincennes

  • Price: €13; free for under-18s and EU residents aged 18 to 25; included in the Paris Museum Pass.
  • Opening hours: daily 10am to 5pm until 20 May, then 10am to 6pm in summer; closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.
  • Sainte-Chapelle: it closes temporarily around midday; visit in the morning or mid-afternoon.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Château de Vincennes

  • Start with the Sainte-Chapelle if you arrive before noon, then move on to the keep.
  • Allow half a day by adding a walk in the Bois de Vincennes around Lac Daumesnil.
  • With children, combine with the Paris Zoo, less than two kilometres away.
