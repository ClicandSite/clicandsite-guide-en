Châtelet-Les Halles, the heart of Paris

The old “belly of Paris” is now the city’s crossroads: a huge glass canopy, a giant church, a contemporary art museum and Rue Montorgueil, the food street.

For eight centuries Les Halles was the great food market of Paris, until it moved out to Rungis in 1969. Today the area is home to the Forum des Halles, topped since 2016 by a vast yellow glass roof, the Canopée, and one of the largest underground stations in the world.

Around it, landmarks and everyday life mix: the church of Saint-Eustache, the Bourse de Commerce turned art museum, pedestrian streets and, a little further north, Rue Montorgueil and its food shops.

A bit of history

Since the Middle Ages, Les Halles was Paris’s great food market, the “belly of Paris” in Émile Zola’s words. In the 19th century Victor Baltard built huge iron-and-glass pavilions there.

The market moved to Rungis in 1969 and the pavilions were demolished in the early 1970s, a decision still regretted. The Forum des Halles opened in 1979 and has been topped by the Canopée since 2016. Nearby, the Fontaine des Innocents dates from the Renaissance.

Things to see at Châtelet-Les Halles

The church of Saint-Eustache

A huge church, Gothic in plan and Renaissance in decoration, famous for its great organ. Free entry. Book a guided tour or activity at Châtelet-Les Halles

The Bourse de Commerce

The old grain exchange, under its dome, now houses the Pinault Collection of contemporary art, redesigned by architect Tadao Ando.

The Canopée and Nelson Mandela garden

The glass roof of the Forum des Halles and the large garden stretching to the Bourse de Commerce.

The Fontaine des Innocents

The oldest monumental fountain in Paris, from the Renaissance, on a lively square.

Rue Montorgueil

A pedestrian street of bakeries, cheese shops, fishmongers and café terraces. The Stohrer pâtisserie has been here since 1730.

The Tour Saint-Jacques

The Flamboyant Gothic bell tower of a vanished church, in its own garden between Châtelet and the Hôtel de Ville.

How to get to Châtelet-Les Halles

By metro and RER

Châtelet (lines 1, 4, 7, 11, 14) and Châtelet-Les Halles (RER A, B and D). Line 14 runs directly to Orly airport.

On foot

The Louvre is ten minutes away along Rue de Rivoli, Le Marais ten minutes to the east.

Practical info 2026: Châtelet-Les Halles

  • Centre Pompidou: closed for renovation since 22 September 2025, with reopening planned for 2030. Its major exhibitions are held at the Grand Palais.
  • Tour Saint-Jacques: closed to visitors until further notice because of works; admire it from the garden.
  • Saint-Eustache: free entry outside services.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Châtelet-Les Halles

  • Châtelet-Les Halles station is a maze: pick your exit (Porte Lescot for the Canopée, Rivoli for the Tour Saint-Jacques) before heading up.
  • For lunch, walk up to Rue Montorgueil rather than staying in the shopping centre.
  • In the evening the Forum area is less pleasant; head for Montorgueil or the riverbanks.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Centre Pompidou open?

No, it has been closed for renovation since September 2025 and is due to reopen in 2030.

What is there to see at Châtelet-Les Halles?

The church of Saint-Eustache, the Bourse de Commerce, the Canopée, the Fontaine des Innocents, Rue Montorgueil and the Tour Saint-Jacques.

Can you climb the Tour Saint-Jacques?

Not at the moment: the tower is closed to visitors until further notice because of works.

How long should you spend at Châtelet-Les Halles?

Allow half a day.

What is there to see near Châtelet-Les Halles?

In this guide, the closest places are the Conciergerie (about 10 min on foot), Louvre (about 10 min on foot) and Sainte-Chapelle (about 15 min on foot).
