Opéra and the Grands Boulevards, Paris for shows and shopping

From the department stores to the 19th-century covered passages, the Grand Rex and the Olympia: the Grands Boulevards have been the place for shopping and nights out for over a century.

The Grands Boulevards form an arc from the Madeleine to Place de la République, by way of the Opéra. Laid out where the old city walls once stood, in the 19th century they became where Parisians went to stroll and be entertained: theatres, cafés, music halls and the first department stores.

The area still plays that role: behind the Palais Garnier stand the Galeries Lafayette and Printemps, and along the boulevards hide 19th-century covered passages with glass roofs, old shops and tea rooms.

A bit of history

The Grands Boulevards follow the line of the old city walls, which Louis XIV had torn down in the 1670s and replaced with a tree-lined promenade; the word “boulevard” itself comes from military vocabulary.

In the 19th century they became the district of theatres, cafés and covered arcades. At the Grand Café on Boulevard des Capucines, the Lumière brothers held the first paying public film screening on 28 December 1895.

Things to see on the Grands Boulevards

The Palais Garnier

The opera house, a Second Empire masterpiece, with its grand staircase and Chagall ceiling. Open for visits during the day. Book a guided tour or activity on the Grands Boulevards

The department stores

Galeries Lafayette and Printemps on Boulevard Haussmann, with their domes and Christmas windows.

Passage des Panoramas

One of the oldest covered passages in Paris, with stamp dealers, engravers and small restaurants.

Passages Jouffroy and Verdeau

Straight across the boulevard: the Musée Grévin, antiquarian bookshops, toys and walking canes.

The Grand Rex

The 1932 Art Deco cinema, one of the largest auditoriums in Europe, with its starry ceiling.

The Olympia and the Madeleine

The most famous music hall in Paris, on Boulevard des Capucines, and the Madeleine church shaped like a Greek temple.

How to get to the Grands Boulevards

By metro and RER

Opéra (lines 3, 7, 8), Chaussée d’Antin-La Fayette (lines 7, 9), Grands Boulevards (lines 8, 9) and Auber (RER A).

On foot

The Louvre is a quarter of an hour away down Avenue de l’Opéra.

Practical info 2026: Opéra and the Grands Boulevards

  • Christmas windows: Printemps Haussmann from 7 November 2026, Galeries Lafayette from 14 November 2026.
  • Covered passages: free to walk through during shop hours; most close in the evening and some on Sundays.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Grands Boulevards

  • Do the passages in a row: Panoramas, then Jouffroy and Verdeau across Boulevard Montmartre.
  • Avoid Boulevard Haussmann on Saturday afternoons in December, when it is packed; come on a weekday morning.
  • For dinner in a period setting, Bouillon Chartier on Rue du Faubourg-Montmartre is a budget-friendly institution.

Frequently asked questions

Which are the most beautiful covered passages in Paris?

Around the Grands Boulevards: Passage des Panoramas, Jouffroy and Verdeau; a little further south, Galerie Vivienne.

Can you visit the Grand Rex?

Most people go to see a film in the great auditorium with its starry ceiling; check the cinema’s website for any tours on offer.

When can I see the Christmas windows?

From 7 November 2026 at Printemps and from 14 November 2026 at Galeries Lafayette.

How long should you spend on the Grands Boulevards?

Allow half a day.

What is there to see near the Grands Boulevards?

In this guide, the closest places are Grévin Wax Museum (about 5 min on foot), Galeries Lafayette (about 15 min on foot) and Palais Garnier (about 15 min on foot).
