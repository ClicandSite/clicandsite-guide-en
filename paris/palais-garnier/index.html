Visiting the Palais Garnier opera house

Marble, gilding and a Chagall ceiling: the Palais Garnier is the most lavish interior in Paris, and you can explore it on your own during the day.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Opened in 1875, the Palais Garnier is the masterpiece of the Second Empire. People come for the grand staircase, the grand foyer, a rival to the Hall of Mirrors, and, when there is no rehearsal, for the auditorium and its ceiling painted by Marc Chagall.

Self-guided visits use a timed ticket, every day during the daytime, except for occasional closures for performances. Suitcases and large bags are not allowed.

Things to do at the Palais Garnier

The grand staircase

A double flight of white marble with onyx banisters and chandeliers: the heart of the building. Book tickets or a guided tour at the Palais Garnier.

The grand foyer

A gilded gallery nearly 50 metres long, with mirrors and painted ceilings.

The auditorium and the Chagall ceiling

Visible from a box when no rehearsal is on; the chandelier weighs several tonnes.

The library-museum

Set models, costumes and memorabilia from the history of the Paris Opera.

The Galeries Lafayette rooftop

Two minutes away, a free rooftop terrace looking over the opera house and all of Paris.

How to get to the Palais Garnier

By metro

Opéra (lines 3, 7 and 8), right outside; RER A at Auber, two minutes away.

On foot

From the Louvre, walk up Avenue de l’Opéra: a quarter of an hour.

Practical info 2026: The Palais Garnier

  • Self-guided visit: €15 for EEA adults, €25 for everyone else; ages 13-25 €10 (EEA) or €20 (non-EEA).
  • Guided tour: €35; 45-minute Flash tour €25 (€15 for EEA visitors).
  • Good to know: suitcases and travel bags are refused and there is no cloakroom during visits. Entrance at the corner of rue Scribe and rue Auber.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Palais Garnier

  • Come early in the morning: the grand staircase is far more photogenic without the crowds.
  • Check the calendar before you go: on some days the auditorium cannot be seen because of rehearsals.
  • The best way to see the auditorium is still to attend a ballet: restricted-view seats are affordable.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit the Palais Garnier without seeing a show?

Yes, self-guided visits are open every day during the daytime, except for occasional closures.

Can you see the Chagall ceiling on the tour?

Yes, from a box, unless a rehearsal or performance is using the auditorium.

How long does a Palais Garnier visit take?

Allow an hour and a half for the public areas and the current exhibition.
