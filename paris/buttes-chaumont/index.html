Buttes-Chaumont, the most romantic park in Paris

A former quarry turned into a mountain landscape: cliffs, a waterfall, a lake and a little temple perched on top of a rock.

Parc des Buttes-Chaumont was laid out under Napoleon III on former gypsum quarries in north-east Paris and opened in 1867 for the Universal Exhibition. The engineers sculpted a genuine mountain scene, with cliffs, a grotto and a lake.

It is one of the Parisians’ favourite parks, very hilly, with sloping lawns for picnics, climbing paths and fine views towards Montmartre and northern Paris.

A bit of history

Before the park, the Buttes-Chaumont was a bare hill riddled with gypsum quarries, close to the old Montfaucon gallows. Napoleon III asked engineer Adolphe Alphand to turn it into an English-style garden.

Opened in 1867 for the World’s Fair, the park owes its cliffs to that past: the lake, the grotto and the Temple of the Sibyl, inspired by the one at Tivoli, were carved out of the former quarry.

Things to see in the Buttes-Chaumont

The Temple de la Sibylle

A small columned temple inspired by Tivoli, on top of a rock overlooking the lake. The view of Paris is superb. Book a guided tour or activity in the Buttes-Chaumont

The lake and the island

The lake surrounds the temple’s rocky island, linked to the banks by a suspension footbridge and a brick bridge.

The grotto and waterfall

A large artificial grotto with stalactites and a waterfall dropping more than twenty metres.

The lawns and open-air cafés

Big sloping lawns and guinguettes where people have a drink on sunny days.

How to get to the Buttes-Chaumont

By metro

Buttes-Chaumont or Botzaris (line 7bis), at the park entrance; Laumière (line 5) for the northern entrance.

On foot

Belleville is ten minutes to the south, La Villette a quarter of an hour to the north.

Practical info 2026: Parc des Buttes-Chaumont

  • Access: free, open during the day and closed at night; hours vary with the season and are posted at the gates.
  • Terrain: the park is very hilly, with steps and steep slopes; wear good shoes.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and the Paris tourist office; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Buttes-Chaumont

  • Head up to the Temple de la Sibylle as soon as you arrive: it is the best viewpoint in the park.
  • Combine with Belleville and the view from Parc de Belleville, ten minutes’ walk away.
  • On a sunny day, bring a picnic: the sloping lawns are made for it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Parc des Buttes-Chaumont free?

Yes, entry is free; it is open during the day and closed at night.

What is there to see in the Buttes-Chaumont?

The Temple de la Sibylle, the lake, the suspension footbridge, the grotto and its waterfall.

How do I get to the Buttes-Chaumont?

By metro to Buttes-Chaumont or Botzaris (line 7bis).

How long should you spend in the Buttes-Chaumont?

Allow one to two hours.

What is there to see near the Buttes-Chaumont?

In this guide, the closest places are Belleville (about 15 min on foot), La Villette (about 25 min on foot) and Canal Saint-Martin (about 30 min on foot).
