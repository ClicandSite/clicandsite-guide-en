The Conciergerie, royal palace and revolutionary prison

On the banks of the Seine, the former palace of the kings of France became the prison of the Terror. Marie Antoinette spent her last days here.

With its pointed towers reflected in the Seine, the Conciergerie is one of the oldest buildings in Paris. It was the heart of the Palais de la Cité, home of the Capetian kings, before becoming a prison in the 14th century.

During the Revolution thousands of prisoners waited here for their trial before the Revolutionary Tribunal. Today you can visit the great Gothic halls and the memorial chapel built where Marie Antoinette’s cell once stood.

What to see at the Conciergerie

The Hall of the Men-at-Arms

A huge early 14th-century Gothic hall, one of the largest in Europe at the time.

Marie Antoinette’s cell

The queen was held here before her execution in 1793; a memorial chapel now stands on the spot.

The HistoPad

A tablet handed out at the entrance brings the rooms back to life in 3D, as they were in the Middle Ages and during the Revolution.

The Women’s Courtyard

The small courtyard where female prisoners gathered during the day.

How to get to the Conciergerie

By metro

Line 4 to Cité, two minutes away on foot.

By RER

RER B or C to Saint-Michel – Notre-Dame.

Address

2 Boulevard du Palais, on Île de la Cité (1st arrondissement).

Tips for visiting the Conciergerie

  • Get the combined ticket with the Sainte-Chapelle next door: it costs less than two separate tickets.
  • Expect security checks at the entrance to the Palais de Justice.
  • Entry is free for under-18s and for EU residents under 26.
  • Notre-Dame is five minutes away on foot: perfect for a morning on Île de la Cité.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Conciergerie cost?

Expect around €13 for an adult in 2026, HistoPad included. The combined ticket with the Sainte-Chapelle saves money. Entry is free for under-18s.

What are the Conciergerie opening hours?

It is open daily from 9.30am to 6pm, with last entry 30 minutes before closing. It is closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

How long does a visit take?

About an hour, a little more if you explore every HistoPad reconstruction.
