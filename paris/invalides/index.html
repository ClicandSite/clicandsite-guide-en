Les Invalides and Napoleon’s tomb

Napoleon I lies beneath the most famous golden dome in Paris. Around it, the Army Museum tells centuries of French military history.

Founded by Louis XIV in 1670 to house wounded soldiers, the Hôtel des Invalides is one of the largest monumental complexes in Paris. Its golden dome towers over the 7th arrondissement, between the Eiffel Tower and the Pont Alexandre III.

Since 1861 the Dôme has held the tomb of Napoleon I, a huge red quartzite sarcophagus. The same ticket covers the Army Museum: armour, uniforms, cannons and galleries on both world wars.

What to see at Les Invalides

Napoleon’s tomb

The sarcophagus sits in a circular crypt under the painted cupola of the Dôme. You look down on it first, then walk around it.

The Army Museum

One of the richest military history museums in the world, from medieval armour to the two world wars.

The main courtyard

A vast courtyard lined with arcades and cannons, at the heart of the building Louis XIV commissioned.

The esplanade

The long lawn runs down to the Seine and the Pont Alexandre III: the finest way to arrive on foot.

How to get to Les Invalides

By metro

Line 8 to La Tour-Maubourg, or line 13 to Varenne.

By RER

RER C to Invalides, across the esplanade.

Address

129 Rue de Grenelle, in the 7th arrondissement.

Tips for visiting Les Invalides

  • Allow at least two hours for the Dôme and part of the Army Museum.
  • On the first Friday of the month the site stays open until 10pm: a lovely chance to see the Dôme in the evening.
  • Entry is free for under-18s, but they still need a ticket.
  • Pair your visit with the Rodin Museum, five minutes away on foot.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a ticket to Les Invalides?

A full-price ticket costs €17 in 2026. It covers the Dôme, Napoleon’s tomb and the Army Museum. Entry is free for under-18s.

What are the opening hours of Les Invalides?

The site is open daily from 10am to 6pm, and until 10pm on the first Friday of the month. It is closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.

Can I see Napoleon’s tomb without visiting the museum?

One ticket covers both: if you are short on time you can stick to the Dôme, which takes about 45 minutes.
