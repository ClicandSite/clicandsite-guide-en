Visiting the Musée de l’Orangerie

Two light-filled oval rooms and eight huge Monet canvases all around you. The Water Lilies are one of the finest moments of any trip to Paris.

At the end of the Tuileries Garden, on the Place de la Concorde side, the Orangerie houses the great Water Lilies cycle that Claude Monet gave to the French state after the First World War. As he wished, the eight panels hang in two oval rooms lit by daylight.

Downstairs, the Walter-Guillaume collection brings together Renoir, Cézanne, Matisse, Picasso, Modigliani and Soutine. To mark one hundred years since Monet’s death, the museum is showing the exhibition “Monet, Painting Time” from 30 September 2026 to 25 January 2027.

A bit of history

The Orangerie was built in 1852 to shelter the orange trees of the Tuileries garden in winter. Just after the 1918 armistice, Claude Monet offered the state a set of large Water Lilies canvases as a tribute to peace.

The two oval rooms designed to hold them opened in 1927, a few months after the painter’s death. The museum also houses the Walter-Guillaume collection, with Renoir, Cézanne, Matisse and Picasso.

Things to do at the Musée de l’Orangerie

The Water Lilies rooms

Nearly 100 metres of canvas showing the Giverny pond from morning to evening. Sit on the central benches and take your time. Book tickets or a guided tour at the Musée de l’Orangerie

The Walter-Guillaume collection

About 150 paintings from Impressionism to modern art, in a short and pleasant circuit.

The “Monet, Painting Time” exhibition

The highlight of winter 2026-2027, on how Monet painted the moment and the passing hours.

The Tuileries Garden

On the way out, a stroll to the Louvre or towards the Place de la Concorde.

How to get to the Musée de l’Orangerie

By metro

Lines 1, 8 and 12, Concorde station, two minutes on foot.

On foot

About 15 minutes from the Louvre through the Tuileries, and just across the Seine from the Musée d’Orsay.

Practical info 2026: The Musée de l’Orangerie

  • Price: €12.50; free on the first Sunday of the month, with a booking. A time slot is required for every visitor, even free ones.
  • Opening hours: 9am–6pm; closed on Tuesdays.
  • Exhibition: “Monet, painting time”, marking 100 years since the painter’s death, 30 September 2026 to 25 January 2027.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Musée de l’Orangerie

  • Book a time slot online: it is required for every visitor, even those entering for free.
  • The museum is closed on Tuesdays. Arrive at 9am opening time to enjoy the Water Lilies almost on your own.
  • On Fridays during exhibitions, the museum often stays open until 9pm with a reduced evening rate.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Orangerie’s opening hours?

Every day except Tuesday, from 9am to 6pm. Closed on 1 May, the morning of 14 July and 25 December.

How much is a ticket?

€12.50 full price. Free for under-18s and under-26s resident in the EU, with a booking.

How long does a visit take?

About 1 hour 30 minutes, or 2 hours with the temporary exhibition.

How long should you spend at the Musée de l’Orangerie?

Allow 1 hour 30 minutes.

What is there to see near the Musée de l’Orangerie?

In this guide, the closest places are Musée d’Orsay (about 10 min on foot), Rodin Museum (about 20 min on foot) and Palais Garnier (about 20 min on foot).
