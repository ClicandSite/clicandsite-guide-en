Visiting the Latin Quarter and the Luxembourg Gardens

The Paris of students and bookshops, from the Panthéon to the Luxembourg Gardens. A neighbourhood to explore on foot, between two cafés.

On the Left Bank, opposite the Île de la Cité, the Latin Quarter is named after the Latin spoken at the Sorbonne in the Middle Ages. Bookshops, art-house cinemas and cafés sit alongside the Panthéon, where Voltaire, Victor Hugo and Marie Curie are buried.

Next door, the Luxembourg Gardens are the Parisians’ favourite park: chestnut-lined paths, the Medici Fountain, toy sailboats on the pond and green chairs free for everyone. Entry is free.

Things to do in the Latin Quarter

The Luxembourg Gardens

The Senate palace, the great pond, the Medici Fountain and over a hundred statues.

The Panthéon

The resting place of great men and women, with Foucault’s pendulum under the dome.

The Sorbonne

The chapel and the fountain on Place de la Sorbonne, lined with cafés.

Rue Mouffetard

A sloping street of food shops, with a market in the mornings from Tuesday to Sunday.

Shakespeare and Company

The famous English-language bookshop facing Notre-Dame. There is often a queue to get in.

The Musée de Cluny

The museum of the Middle Ages, with the Lady and the Unicorn tapestries and Gallo-Roman baths.

How to get to the Latin Quarter

By RER and metro

Luxembourg (RER B) for the gardens, Cluny - La Sorbonne (line 10) or Saint-Michel (line 4, RER B and C) for the quarter.

On foot

From Notre-Dame, cross the Petit-Pont: five minutes to Rue Saint-Jacques.

Tips for visiting the Latin Quarter

  • Start with Rue Mouffetard in the morning, walk up to the Panthéon, then finish in the Luxembourg Gardens.
  • With children: sailboats on the pond, the puppet theatre and the playground in the Luxembourg.
  • The gardens’ hours follow the sun: they close earlier in winter.

Frequently asked questions

Are the Luxembourg Gardens free?

Yes, entry is free every day. Opening hours vary with the season.

Who is buried in the Panthéon?

Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Émile Zola, Marie Curie and Josephine Baker, among others.

How do I get to the Luxembourg Gardens?

Take RER B to Luxembourg station, right by the main entrance.
