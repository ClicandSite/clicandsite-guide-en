Visiting the Panthéon

Victor Hugo, Marie Curie, Josephine Baker: under the great dome of the Latin Quarter, France honours the people who shaped it.

Built in the 18th century as a church dedicated to Saint Genevieve, the Panthéon became, during the Revolution, the place where the nation honours its great figures. Its dome towers over the Montagne Sainte-Geneviève, in the heart of the Latin Quarter.

A visit covers the nave, with the famous Foucault pendulum, and the crypt, where Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Émile Zola, Marie Curie, Louis Braille, Josephine Baker and Robert Badinter lie, among others. From 25 September 2026 to 31 January 2027, the nave also hosts the “Vies minuscules” exhibition at no extra charge.

A bit of history

Louis XV, after recovering from a serious illness in 1744, vowed to rebuild the church dedicated to Saint Genevieve. Architect Soufflot designed a neoclassical building, completed after his death.

In 1791 the Revolution turned it into a republican temple for great figures. Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Marie Curie and Josephine Baker are buried here. In 1851 Léon Foucault demonstrated the Earth’s rotation here with his famous pendulum.

Things to do at the Panthéon

Foucault’s pendulum

In 1851 Léon Foucault demonstrated the Earth’s rotation here with a pendulum hung from the dome. A copy still swings in the middle of the nave. Book tickets or a guided tour at the Panthéon

The crypt

A long maze of vaulted galleries where more than 80 figures are buried, from Enlightenment philosophers to Resistance heroes.

The nave paintings

Large 19th-century murals tell the life of Saint Genevieve and the history of France.

The upper levels

From April to October you can climb about 200 steps to the colonnade for a 360° view of Paris (extra charge).

How to get to the Panthéon

By RER

RER B, Luxembourg station, about eight minutes on foot.

By metro

Line 10, Cardinal Lemoine or Maubert-Mutualité station.

On foot

A few minutes from the Luxembourg Gardens and the Sorbonne: easy to combine with a walk around the Latin Quarter.

Practical info 2026: The Panthéon

  • Price: €13 to €16 depending on the season (the lower price applies October to March); free for under-18s.
  • Exhibition: “Vies minuscules”, with the Centre Pompidou, under the nave from 24 September 2026 to 31 January 2027.
  • Opening hours: daily 10 am to 6 pm from October to March (6.30 pm April to September), last entry 45 minutes before closing; opens at noon on the first working Monday of the month. Closed 1 January, 1 May and 25 December; free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month, November to March.

Prices and opening hours checked in November 2026 with the official sites and the Paris tourist office; they can change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting the Panthéon

  • Buy your ticket online: it is slightly cheaper than at the desk and saves queuing.
  • Allow an hour for the nave and crypt, longer if you see the exhibition.
  • Entry is free for under-18s, and for 18 to 25-year-olds resident in the European Union.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Panthéon’s opening hours?

From October to March, daily from 10am to 6pm; from April to September until 6.30pm. Last entry 45 minutes before closing.

How much does the Panthéon cost?

About €13 online for an adult. Free for under-18s and for 18 to 25-year-olds resident in the EU.

Who is buried in the Panthéon?

Among others Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Émile Zola, Jean Moulin, Marie Curie, Louis Braille, Simone Veil, Josephine Baker and Robert Badinter.

How long should you spend at the Panthéon?

Allow 1 hour.

What is there to see near the Panthéon?
