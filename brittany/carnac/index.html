Carnac, the town of standing stones

Nearly three thousand stones raised more than six thousand years ago, lined up over kilometres: the Carnac alignments are one of the largest megalithic sites in the world.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Carnac, in the Morbihan, is famous for its alignments: thousands of standing stones raised in the Neolithic, in parallel rows stretching for several kilometres. Their purpose is still a mystery, which only adds to the appeal.

The town is also a seaside resort with long sandy beaches, close to the Quiberon peninsula and the Gulf of Morbihan.

Things to see in Carnac

The Ménec alignments

The largest of the three groups, with more than a thousand stones. See guided tours and things to do in Carnac

The Kermario and Kerlescan alignments

The other two great rows of stones, to explore on foot or by bike.

The Maison des Mégalithes

The visitor centre, with explanations and the starting point for guided tours.

The Saint-Michel tumulus

A large burial mound topped by a chapel, with views over the bay.

Carnac’s beaches

The Grande Plage and its neighbours, on the Bay of Quiberon.

How to get to Carnac

By train

TGV to Auray, then bus or taxi to Carnac, about fifteen kilometres away.

By car

About 1 hr 30 from Rennes, 45 minutes from Vannes.

Practical info 2026: Carnac

  • Alignments: in winter, some of the sites are open freely; in the warmer months, access to the heart of the alignments is by guided tour, booked at the Maison des Mégalithes.
  • Walking: a path runs alongside the alignments, on foot or by bike.

Tips for visiting Carnac

  • Come in winter or early in the morning to walk the alignments almost alone.
  • Hire a bike: the three groups follow one another over about four kilometres.
  • Carry on to the Quiberon peninsula and its wild coast, twenty minutes away.

Frequently asked questions

How many standing stones are there at Carnac?

Nearly three thousand, mainly across the Ménec, Kermario and Kerlescan alignments.

Can you walk among the stones?

In winter, some of the alignments are open freely; the rest of the year, access to the heart of the sites is by guided tour.

How do I get to Carnac without a car?

By train to Auray, then by bus or taxi.
