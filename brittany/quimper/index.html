Quimper, in the heart of Cornouaille

A Gothic cathedral with soaring spires, half-timbered houses and flowered footbridges over the Odet: Quimper is one of the most charming towns in Finistère.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Quimper, the capital of Finistère, is the historic capital of Cornouaille, where the Odet and the Steir meet. Its pedestrian old town is laid out around Saint-Corentin cathedral, with streets of half-timbered houses and lively squares.

The town is also the home of Quimper faience, hand-painted for more than three centuries, and one of the capitals of the Breton crêpe.

Things to see in Quimper

Saint-Corentin cathedral

A twin-spired Gothic cathedral with a slightly skewed nave and fine stained glass. See guided tours and things to do in Quimper

The old town

Place au Beurre, Rue Kéréon and their half-timbered houses, pedestrianised and full of shops.

The banks of the Odet

The quays and flowered footbridges crossing the river right in the centre.

The Musée des Beaux-Arts

A fine painting collection, including works from the Pont-Aven school.

Quimper faience

The faience workshops and shops, with hand-painted Breton designs.

How to get to Quimper

By train

Direct TGV from Paris-Montparnasse in about 4 hrs.

Nearby

Concarneau is half an hour away, the Pointe du Raz about an hour.

Practical info 2026: Quimper

  • Old town: largely pedestrianised; park at the car parks on the edge of the centre.
  • Festival de Cornouaille: a big celebration of Breton culture every July.

Tips for visiting Quimper

  • Try a wheat crêpe or a buckwheat galette in the old town, where the tradition is strongest.
  • Climb Mont Frugy, above the Odet, for the view of the cathedral.
  • Combine with Concarneau and its walled town, half an hour away.
