Saint-Malo, the corsair city

A fortified town set in the sea, ramparts to walk along and islets you can reach on foot at low tide: Saint-Malo is the essential stop on the Emerald Coast.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Saint-Malo, in Ille-et-Vilaine, grew rich from its shipowners and corsairs, Surcouf among them. Its walled town, known as Intra-Muros, is surrounded by nearly two kilometres of ramparts. Largely destroyed in 1944, it was rebuilt exactly as before after the war.

The sea sets the pace of a visit: at low tide you can walk out to the Grand Bé, where Chateaubriand is buried, and to Fort National. The tides at Saint-Malo are among the highest in Europe.

Things to see in Saint-Malo

The ramparts walk

A walk of about two kilometres above the sea and beaches, free and open all year. See guided tours and things to do in Saint-Malo.

Intra-Muros

The granite streets of the walled town, Saint-Vincent cathedral and the crêperies.

The Grand Bé and Fort National

Two islets you can walk to at low tide: Chateaubriand’s grave on one, Vauban’s fort on the other.

Sillon beach

The long beach running towards Paramé, with its wooden breakwaters.

Saint-Servan and the Tour Solidor

The neighbouring district, its coves and its 14th-century tower on the Rance.

The Grand Aquarium

A large aquarium with a shark ring, popular in bad weather.

How to get to Saint-Malo

By train

Direct TGV from Paris-Montparnasse in about 2 hrs 15; the station is twenty minutes’ walk from Intra-Muros.

Nearby

Dinan is half an hour’s drive away, Mont-Saint-Michel about three quarters of an hour.

Practical info 2026: Saint-Malo

  • Tides: the Grand Bé and Fort National can only be reached at low tide; check the tide times and come back before the sea rises.
  • Ramparts: free access all year round.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Saint-Malo

  • Walk the ramparts late in the day, when the light falls over the sea.
  • Check the tide times before heading out to the Grand Bé: the sea comes back fast.
  • Combine with Dinan, half an hour away.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to walk Saint-Malo’s ramparts?

About an hour on foot for the two kilometres or so of ramparts, free and open all year.

How do I get to the Grand Bé?

On foot, only at low tide, from Bon-Secours beach; check the tide times.

How do I get to Saint-Malo from Paris?

By direct TGV from Paris-Montparnasse, in about 2 hrs 15.
