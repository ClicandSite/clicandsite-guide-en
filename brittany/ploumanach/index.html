Ploumanac’h, on the Pink Granite Coast

Strangely shaped pink granite rocks polished by sea and wind, a lighthouse among the boulders and one of the finest coastal paths in France.

Ploumanac’h is a small harbour in the commune of Perros-Guirec, in the Côtes-d’Armor. It has given its name to one of Brittany’s most astonishing landscapes: a jumble of pink granite boulders carved by erosion into the shapes of a tortoise, a hat or a skull.

The customs officers’ path, part of the GR 34, winds among these boulders between Trestraou beach and Ploumanac’h harbour, passing below the Mean Ruz lighthouse.

Things to see in Ploumanac’h

The customs officers’ path

A walk of about five kilometres between Perros-Guirec and Ploumanac’h, among the pink rocks. See guided tours and things to do in Ploumanac’h and the Pink Granite Coast.

The Mean Ruz lighthouse

The pink granite lighthouse set among the rocks, the emblem of the coast.

The harbour and the Saint-Guirec oratory

Little Saint-Guirec beach and its oratory on a rock, cut off at high tide.

The Sept-Îles

An offshore nature reserve where gannets and puffins nest, seen by boat.

Trégastel and Trébeurden

The neighbouring towns, with more rock formations and fine beaches.

How to get to Ploumanac’h

By train

TGV to Lannion via Guingamp, then bus or taxi to Perros-Guirec.

By car

About 2 hrs 15 from Rennes; paid car parks near the path in season.

Practical info 2026: Ploumanac’h and the Pink Granite Coast

  • Customs officers’ path: free access all year; stay on the marked path, the vegetation is fragile.
  • Sept-Îles: you cannot land on the reserve’s islands; boat trips go around them.

Tips for visiting Ploumanac’h

  • Walk the path late in the day: the granite turns orange at sunset.
  • Bring good shoes and a windproof jacket, even in summer.
  • Combine with Saint-Malo or Dinan for a tour of the north coast.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Pink Granite Coast?

On Brittany’s north coast, in the Côtes-d’Armor, between Perros-Guirec, Ploumanac’h, Trégastel and Trébeurden.

How long is the customs officers’ path?

About two hours there and back for the five kilometres between Perros-Guirec and Ploumanac’h.

Can you visit the Sept-Îles?

You cannot land on the reserve, but boats sail around it from Perros-Guirec.
