Corte lies in the centre of Corsica, between Ajaccio and Bastia, where the Tavignano and Restonica rivers meet. Its citadel, perched on a rocky peak, is the only one on the island built inland.

A university town and a town of history, Corte is also the starting point for many mountain hikes.

A bit of history

In 1755 Pasquale Paoli made Corte the capital of independent Corsica and founded a university there in 1765. The Università di Corsica reopened in 1981, making Corte the island’s student town.

What to see in Corte

The citadel and eagle’s nest

The fortress and its lookout, with views over the valleys.

The Museum of Corsica

The regional anthropology museum, in the citadel’s former barracks.

The old town

Its steep lanes, Place Gaffory and the statue of Pasquale Paoli.

The Restonica valley

Gorges and clear pools, with the Melo and Capitello lakes at the end of the road.

How to get to Corte

By train

Corte is on the Ajaccio-Bastia line of the Corsican railways.

By car

About 1 hour 15 from Bastia and 1 hour 45 from Ajaccio on the main road.

Restonica

The valley road is narrow; in summer, access to the upper valley is regulated.

Practical information 2026: Corte

  • Museum of Corsica: €5.50 with temporary exhibitions, €4 without; the ticket also covers the citadel.
  • Old town: free to explore.
  • Hiking: set off early for the Restonica lakes and carry water.

Our tips for visiting Corte

  • Climb to the eagle’s nest late in the day for the light on the mountains.
  • Swim in the Restonica pools in summer.
  • Take the train from Ajaccio or Bastia for the scenery.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Museum of Corsica?

€5.50 with temporary exhibitions, or €4 without; the ticket includes the citadel.

How do you get to Corte without a car?

By train on the Ajaccio-Bastia line, which stops at Corte.

What is there to do around Corte?

Head up the Restonica valley to the Melo and Capitello lakes.
