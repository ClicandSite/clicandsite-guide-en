Bonifacio sits at the southern tip of Corsica, facing the Strait of Bonifacio and Sardinia. The upper town, ringed by ramparts, is perched on white limestone cliffs that plunge into the sea.

Explore the upper town on foot, then head down to the marina for a boat trip along the cliffs or out to the Lavezzi Islands. Allow a day.

A bit of history

A strategic stronghold on the strait, Bonifacio passed to Genoa at the end of the 12th century and became one of its most important citadels. In 1420 the town withstood a long siege by Alfonso V, King of Aragon.

Legend says his troops carved the staircase that bears his name in a single night to surprise the garrison. The upper town has kept its ramparts, narrow lanes and houses perched on the cliff.

Things to see in Bonifacio

The upper town

Narrow lanes, tall houses and ramparts, with plunging views of the sea. See boat trips and tours in Bonifacio

The King of Aragon’s Staircase

A staircase of about 187 steps carved into the cliff, leading down to sea level.

The marine cemetery

At the end of the plateau, its white chapels face the sea.

The cliffs by boat

From the marina, trips follow the cliffs, sea caves and the Grain de Sable rock.

The Lavezzi Islands

The archipelago, about ten kilometres away, is reached by boat from the port.

How to get to Bonifacio

By air

Figari airport is about twenty kilometres away.

By car

About 30 minutes from Porto-Vecchio; car parks are paid and fill up fast in summer.

By ferry

Crossings to Santa Teresa Gallura in Sardinia.

Tips for visiting Bonifacio

  • Come early to find a parking space in summer.
  • Wear good shoes for the King of Aragon’s Staircase.
  • Take the boat trip late in the day for the light on the cliffs.
