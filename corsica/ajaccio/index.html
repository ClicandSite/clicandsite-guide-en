Ajaccio, the imperial city on its gulf

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 13 November 2026

Ajaccio is the largest town in Corsica and the seat of the Corsican regional government, at the head of a gulf on the west coast. Napoleon Bonaparte was born here on 15 August 1769, and the town bears his mark everywhere.

The old town takes half a day on foot, between the Maison Bonaparte, the cathedral and the port. Keep the end of the day for the Route des Sanguinaires, which follows the coast to the Pointe de la Parata.

A bit of history

Ajaccio was founded in 1492 by the Genoese, who built a citadel to control the gulf. Napoleon Bonaparte was born here on 15 August 1769, a year after Corsica passed to France.

Made the capital of Corsica under the Empire, the town honours the emperor everywhere, from his birthplace to Place d’Austerlitz. Today it is the seat of the Corsican regional government.

Things to see in Ajaccio

The Maison Bonaparte

Napoleon’s birthplace on Rue Saint-Charles is now a national museum devoted to the Bonaparte family. See tours and boat trips in Ajaccio

The old town and cathedral

Lanes of colourful façades around the Cathedral of Notre-Dame-de-l’Assomption, where Napoleon was baptised.

The Palais Fesch

The fine arts museum founded by Cardinal Fesch, Napoleon’s uncle, with a major collection of Italian paintings.

The citadel and harbour

The Genoese citadel lines the sea, a short walk from the old port and its fishing boats.

La Parata and the Sanguinaires

About twelve kilometres away, a Genoese tower overlooks the Sanguinaires islands, beautiful at sunset.

How to get to Ajaccio

By air

Ajaccio airport is a few kilometres from the centre, linked by bus.

By ferry

Regular crossings from Marseille, Toulon and Nice.

By car

About 3 hours from Bastia and 2 h 45 from Bonifacio, on winding roads.

  • Opening times: April to September, 10 am to 12.30 pm and 1.15 pm to 5.30 pm; shorter hours in winter.

Tips for visiting Ajaccio

  • Go to La Parata late in the day for sunset over the Sanguinaires.
  • Visit the Maison Bonaparte in the morning, before the heat.
  • The market near the port is ideal for tasting Corsican produce.
