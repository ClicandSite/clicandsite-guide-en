Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, the source of the Sorgue

At the end of a ring of cliffs, a whole river comes out of the ground: one of the largest springs in France.

Fontaine-de-Vaucluse gave its name to the département. At the end of the village, at the foot of a cliff more than 200 metres high, a chasm releases the Sorgue, an intensely green river that then flows through the village.

Divers and robots have explored the chasm to more than 300 metres deep without finding the bottom.

A bit of history

The Italian poet Petrarch lived in Fontaine-de-Vaucluse in the 14th century; a museum-library is dedicated to him. The village also long lived from its mills, including a paper mill that still works.

What to see in Fontaine-de-Vaucluse

The Sorgue chasm

A 15-minute path follows the river to the spring. In spring the water overflows and boils; in summer the level drops and you can see the pool.

The riverside village

Terraces, water wheels and the green water of the Sorgue.

The paper mill

Paper is still made by hand there, using old techniques.

The castle

The ruins of a medieval castle above the village, with a view over the valley.

How to get to Fontaine-de-Vaucluse

By car

About 30 minutes from Avignon and 10 minutes from L’Isle-sur-la-Sorgue. Paid car parks at the entrance to the village.

By bike

A small road follows the Sorgue from L’Isle-sur-la-Sorgue to Fontaine-de-Vaucluse.

On a tour

Day trips from Avignon combine Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Gordes and Roussillon.

Practical info 2026: Fontaine-de-Vaucluse

  • Path to the spring: free access.
  • Parking: paid car parks at the entrance to the village.
  • Best season: spring, when the spring overflows.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Fontaine-de-Vaucluse

  • Come in spring to see the spring in full flow.
  • Arrive early in summer: the village is small and very busy.
  • Do not climb down into the chasm, the rocks are slippery.
  • Combine with L’Isle-sur-la-Sorgue and its Sunday market.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to see the Fontaine-de-Vaucluse spring?

In spring, after the rains, when the spring overflows. In summer the level drops sharply.

Is access to the spring free?

Yes, the path to the chasm is free; only the car parks charge.

How long do you need in Fontaine-de-Vaucluse?

Two to three hours is enough for the spring and the village.
