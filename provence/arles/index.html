Arles, the Rome of Provence

A Roman arena still in use, the places Van Gogh painted and a steel tower by Frank Gehry: Arles mixes two thousand years of history on the banks of the Rhône.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Arles, on the Rhône at the gateway to the Camargue, was one of the great cities of Roman Gaul. Its Roman and Romanesque monuments are UNESCO World Heritage: the arena, the ancient theatre, the cryptoporticus, the baths and Saint-Trophime cloister.

Vincent van Gogh lived here in 1888 and 1889 and painted more than three hundred works. More recently, Frank Gehry’s LUMA tower has made Arles a contemporary art destination, alongside the Rencontres photography festival every summer.

Things to see in Arles

The arena

The 1st-century Roman amphitheatre, still used for Camargue bull games and shows. Book a guided tour of Roman Arles and Van Gogh’s town

The ancient theatre

The tiers and two surviving columns of the Roman theatre, a stone’s throw from the arena.

Saint-Trophime cloister

The church’s sculpted portal and one of the finest Romanesque cloisters in Provence.

The Alyscamps

The old Roman necropolis lined with sarcophagi, painted by Van Gogh and Gauguin.

In Van Gogh’s footsteps

The café on Place du Forum, the Espace Van Gogh and the Fondation Vincent van Gogh.

The LUMA tower

Frank Gehry’s shimmering steel tower and its park, devoted to contemporary art.

How to get to Arles

By train

TGV to Avignon, then TER to Arles in about twenty minutes; a few direct trains from Paris.

Nearby

Les Baux-de-Provence is half an hour away, Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer about forty minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What is there to see in Arles?

The arena, the ancient theatre, Saint-Trophime cloister, the Alyscamps, Van Gogh’s places and the LUMA tower.

How long did Van Gogh live in Arles?

About fifteen months, from February 1888 to May 1889, during which he painted more than three hundred works.

How do I get to Arles from Paris?

By TGV to Avignon, then TER to Arles, or on one of the few direct trains.
