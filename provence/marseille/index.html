Marseille, from the Old Port to the Bonne Mère

France’s oldest city, with its harbour, its hilltop basilica and its lively neighbourhoods: Marseille is worth visiting all year round, and winter is often sunny.

Founded by the Greeks 2,600 years ago, Marseille centres on the Old Port, overlooked by Notre-Dame de la Garde, the “Bonne Mère”. The basilica is free and open every day, with the best view over the city and the Frioul islands.

From 14 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, France’s oldest santon fair sets up on the Old Port quay. The MuCEM is open every day except Tuesday.

What to see in Marseille

Notre-Dame de la Garde

The neo-Byzantine basilica on the hilltop, its mosaics and its 360° panorama.

The Old Port

The heart of the city, with its morning fish market and Norman Foster’s mirror canopy.

Le Panier

Marseille’s oldest quarter, its colourful lanes and the Vieille Charité.

The MuCEM and Fort Saint-Jean

The museum of Mediterranean civilisations, linked to the fort by a footbridge.

How to get to Marseille

By TGV

About 3 h 15 from Paris to Saint-Charles station, right in the centre.

By air

Marseille-Provence airport, linked to Saint-Charles station by shuttle bus.

To Notre-Dame de la Garde

Bus 60 from the Old Port, or a 30-minute walk uphill.

Our tips for visiting Marseille

  • Go up to Notre-Dame de la Garde in the morning, when the view is clearest.
  • Take the shuttle boat to the Château d’If and the Frioul islands.
  • Try bouillabaisse at a restaurant that follows the charter, and panisse at the Old Port.
  • Marseille is the gateway to the calanques, 30 minutes away by road.

Frequently asked questions

Is Notre-Dame de la Garde free to visit?

Yes, the basilica is free. It is open every day, from 7 am to about 6.15 pm in low season.

When is the Marseille santon fair?

In 2026, from 14 November to 3 January 2027 on the Old Port quay. Entry is free.

Which day is the MuCEM closed?

The MuCEM is closed on Tuesdays, and on 1 May and 25 December.
