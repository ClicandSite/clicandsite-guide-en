Bayonne, capital of the Basque Country

Tall houses with colourful shutters along the Nive, a Gothic cathedral and a chocolate tradition almost four centuries old.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Bayonne grew up where the Adour and the Nive meet. Grand Bayonne, around the cathedral, and Petit Bayonne, across the Nive, form a lively old centre of narrow houses painted red, green or blue.

The town is also the gourmet capital of the Basque Country: chocolate makers have been here since the 17th century, and Bayonne ham bears its name.

Things to see in Bayonne

The Nive quays

Colourful half-timbered houses reflected in the river, with their terraces. Book a guided tour or tasting in Bayonne.

Sainte-Marie cathedral

A Gothic cathedral with soaring spires and a cloister, on the Way of St James.

The Basque Museum

The great museum of Basque culture, in Petit Bayonne.

The chocolate makers

The historic shops of Rue du Port-Neuf, for a hot chocolate or a tasting.

The market hall

The covered market on the Nive, for ham, cheese and Basque produce.

How to get to Bayonne

By train

Direct TGV from Paris-Montparnasse in about 4 hours.

Nearby

Biarritz is a quarter of an hour away, Espelette half an hour.

Practical info 2026: Bayonne

  • Fêtes de Bayonne: the biggest festival in France, every summer at the end of July, everyone dressed in white and red.
  • Old centre: largely pedestrianised; park at the car parks on the edge.

Prices and opening times checked in November 2026 with official websites and local tourist offices; they may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Bayonne

  • Have a frothy hot chocolate at one of the historic chocolate makers on Rue du Port-Neuf.
  • Visit the market hall in the morning for ham and sheep’s cheese.
  • Combine with Biarritz in the afternoon.
