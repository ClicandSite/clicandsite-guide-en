Sare is a village in Labourd, in the hills behind Saint-Jean-de-Luz, right on the Spanish border. It is listed among the most beautiful villages of France.

Its Basque houses with red timbers, its square with the pelota court and its church with wooden galleries make it a classic Basque village. La Rhune, which towers above it, is climbed by rack railway from the Col de Saint-Ignace, within the village boundaries.

A bit of history

A border village, Sare long lived from farming and smuggling with neighbouring Spain. The Saint-Martin church has kept its three tiers of wooden galleries, typical of Basque churches.

What to see in Sare

The square and pelota court

The heart of the village, where Basque pelota is played.

Saint-Martin church

Wooden galleries inside and disc-shaped gravestones in the churchyard.

The Sare caves

A cave system to visit with the family, in a protected natural site.

La Rhune rack railway

It runs from the Col de Saint-Ignace to the summit, with views of the ocean and the Pyrenees.

How to get to Sare

By car

About 25 minutes from Saint-Jean-de-Luz.

By bus

A regular bus links Saint-Jean-de-Luz and Sare.

On foot

Many paths lead from the village to La Rhune and the hills.

Practical information 2026: Sare

  • La Rhune railway: seasonal; check the opening dates before you come.

Our tips for visiting Sare

  • Book the caves before you come: places per tour are limited.
  • Go up La Rhune early in the morning, before the crowds and the clouds.
  • Try Basque cake in one of the village bakeries.
