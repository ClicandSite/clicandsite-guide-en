Espelette, the pepper village

White houses with red shutters, garlands of peppers drying on the façades and Basque hills all around: Espelette is the most famous of the inland villages.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 12 November 2026

Espelette is a village in the Labourd, at the foot of the Basque mountains, half an hour from Bayonne. It has given its name to the pepper grown in about ten surrounding communes, protected by a designation of origin.

In late summer and autumn, the peppers dry in long red garlands on the white house fronts, making Espelette one of the most photographed villages in the Basque Country.

Things to see in Espelette

The pepper-hung façades

Garlands of peppers drying on the houses, especially from late summer to autumn. Book a trip to the Basque villages around Espelette

The barons’ castle

The village castle, home to the town hall and the tourist office.

Shops and workshops

Ground pepper, jellies, pepper chocolate and Basque produce.

Saint-Étienne church

A typical Basque church with wooden galleries and a cemetery of disc-shaped headstones.

Ainhoa and Sare

Two more of the loveliest villages in the Basque Country, a few kilometres away.

How to get to Espelette

By car

About half an hour from Bayonne or Biarritz; paid car parks in season.

By bus

Buses link Bayonne and Espelette, with a reduced service at weekends.

Practical info 2026: Espelette

  • Pepper festival: every year at the end of October, with a market and events.
  • Espelette pepper: protected by an AOP, grown in about ten communes around the village.

Tips for visiting Espelette

  • Come in autumn to see the house fronts covered in peppers.
  • Carry on to Ainhoa and Sare, less than twenty minutes away, for a loop of Basque villages.
  • Combine with Bayonne and its chocolate makers.

Frequently asked questions

When can you see the peppers on Espelette’s houses?

When is the pepper festival?

Every year at the end of October.

Which villages are near Espelette?
