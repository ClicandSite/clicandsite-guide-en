Collioure, the painters’ harbour

A bell tower standing in the sea, a royal castle, Catalan boats: the village where Matisse and Derain invented Fauvism.

Collioure is a small Catalan harbour in the Pyrénées-Orientales, where the Pyrenees plunge into the Mediterranean. Its colourful houses, church with a bell tower standing in the water and royal castle make it one of the prettiest villages on the coast.

It is also one of the most popular spring destinations in the south of France: the weather is mild and the summer crowds have not yet arrived.

A bit of history

In 1905 Henri Matisse and André Derain spent the summer in Collioure. Their pure-coloured canvases painted here gave birth to Fauvism. The Fauvism trail in the streets shows copies of their paintings where they were painted.

What to see in Collioure

The royal castle

A fortress in the middle of the bay, watching over the town since the 13th century, with exhibitions.

Notre-Dame-des-Anges

The church and its round bell tower, a former lighthouse, by the water: the symbol of Collioure.

The Fauvism trail

About twenty copies of Matisse and Derain paintings placed around town.

Fort Saint-Elme and the windmill

A climb through the vines to the windmill, with a view over the whole bay.

Anchovies and wines

Collioure anchovies and Collioure and Banyuls wines, to taste in the cellars.

How to get to Collioure

By train

Collioure has a station on the Perpignan – Cerbère line, about twenty minutes from Perpignan.

By car

Via the A9 then the D914; parking is hard in summer, use the car parks at the edge of town.

On foot

The coastal path links Collioure and Port-Vendres in about an hour.

Tips for visiting Collioure

  • Come in spring or September: it is mild and quieter.
  • Come by train in summer to avoid parking problems.
  • Walk up to the windmill for the best view of the bay.
  • Combine with Banyuls and the Côte Vermeille.
