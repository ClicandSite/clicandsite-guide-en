The Côte Vermeille is the last stretch of French coast before Spain: about twenty kilometres of rocky shore where the Pyrenees plunge into the sea, between Collioure and Cerbère.

It is named after the colour of its rocks at sunset. In spring it is one of the best-value destinations in the south of France, before the high season.

What to see on the Côte Vermeille

Collioure

The painters’ harbour, with its royal castle and seaside bell tower.

Port-Vendres

A working fishing port, with its fish market and quayside restaurants.

Banyuls-sur-Mer

The town of sweet wine, the marine reserve and the sculptor Aristide Maillol, who was born there.

The Cerbère-Banyuls marine reserve

France’s first marine nature reserve, with a marked underwater trail at Peyrefite beach in summer.

The terraced vineyards

Dry-stone walls running down to the sea, to explore on foot or by 4x4.

How to get to the Côte Vermeille

By train

The Perpignan – Cerbère line serves Collioure, Port-Vendres, Banyuls and Cerbère: ideal for the coast without a car.

By car

Along the coastal D914 and then D86, winding and superb.

On foot

The coastal path links the villages over the headlands and coves.

Practical info 2026: the Côte Vermeille

  • Beaches and paths: free access.
  • Peyrefite underwater trail: summer only.
  • Parking: difficult in the villages in summer: take the train.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for the Côte Vermeille

  • Come in spring: it is mild, prices are lower and the villages quieter.
  • Take the train between villages and walk a stretch of the coastal path.
  • Try Banyuls wine and Collioure anchovies.
  • Pack a mask and snorkel for the marine reserve in summer.

Frequently asked questions

Which villages should you see on the Côte Vermeille?

Collioure, Port-Vendres, Banyuls-sur-Mer and Cerbère, from north to south.

Can you do the Côte Vermeille without a car?

Yes, the Perpignan – Cerbère train stops in every village on the coast.

When should you go to the Côte Vermeille?

In spring and September, for mild weather and lower prices than in summer.
