Lourdes lies in the Hautes-Pyrénées, on the Gave de Pau river. It is one of the largest Catholic pilgrimage sites in the world, around the Massabielle grotto.

Believer or not, visitors also come for the atmosphere of the torchlight procession, the castle and the mountains close by.

A bit of history

In 1858 a young girl from Lourdes, Bernadette Soubirous, said she had seen the Virgin Mary eighteen times in the Massabielle grotto. The sanctuary grew up around the grotto, with three basilicas built one above or beside the other. Pope Leo XIV visited in September 2026.

What to see in Lourdes

The Massabielle grotto

The site of the apparitions, on the riverbank, where thousands of candles burn.

The basilicas

The Basilica of the Immaculate Conception, the Rosary Basilica and the underground Saint Pius X Basilica.

The torchlight procession

Every evening at 9 pm in season, thousands of pilgrims walk with candles.

Pic du Jer

A funicular built in 1900 climbs to the summit, with a view over Lourdes and the Pyrenees.

How to get to Lourdes

By train

Lourdes is served by TGV and night trains from Paris.

By plane

Tarbes-Lourdes-Pyrénées airport is about ten kilometres away.

Getting around

The sanctuary is fifteen minutes on foot from the station.

Practical information 2026: Lourdes

  • Sanctuary: free access.
  • Torchlight procession: every evening at 9 pm, from 5 April to 1 November 2026.
  • Pic du Jer: funicular €14.50 return for an adult in 2026; open until 1 November 2026.

Our tips for visiting Lourdes

  • Attend the torchlight procession, even if you are not a pilgrim.
  • Avoid 15 August and the big pilgrimages if you want peace and quiet.
  • Make time for the Cirque de Gavarnie or the Pont d’Espagne.
