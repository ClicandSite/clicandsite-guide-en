Bordeaux, the sleeping beauty awake

Golden 18th-century façades, riverside quays given back to pedestrians and a wine museum on the Garonne: Bordeaux is best explored on foot over two days.

Bordeaux stretches along a bend in the Garonne, the “Port of the Moon”, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2007. Its quays, long lined with warehouses, have become a long promenade where people come in the evening to see Place de la Bourse reflected in the Water Mirror.

The centre is easy to cover on foot or by tram, from Place de la Bourse to the Grand-Théâtre and the cathedral, then north to the Chartrons and the Cité du Vin.

A bit of history

Bordeaux began as Burdigala, a Gallic then Roman port on the Garonne. In 1152 Eleanor of Aquitaine married Henry Plantagenet, the future king of England, bringing the city under the English crown for three centuries, when Bordeaux “claret” was shipped to England in bulk.

Back under French rule from 1453, the city enjoyed its golden age in the 18th century thanks to maritime trade: Place de la Bourse and the Grand-Théâtre date from that time. The “Port of the Moon” has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2007.

Things to see in Bordeaux

Place de la Bourse and the Water Mirror

The grand 18th-century royal square facing the river, with a thin sheet of water in front that alternates reflections and mist: Bordeaux’s signature photo. See guided tours and wine tastings in Bordeaux

The Grand-Théâtre and the Golden Triangle

Victor Louis’s colonnaded opera house, opened in 1780, surrounded by the smart streets of Cours de l’Intendance and Allées de Tourny.

Saint-André cathedral and Pey-Berland tower

A great Gothic cathedral and its detached bell tower, which you can climb for views over the rooftops.

Rue Sainte-Catherine and old Bordeaux

A long pedestrian shopping street, then the lanes of the Saint-Pierre district, Porte Cailhau and the Grosse Cloche.

The Chartrons and the Cité du Vin

The old wine merchants’ district with its antique shops and Sunday market, then the Cité du Vin, a museum of wine cultures, with a glass on the rooftop.

How to get to Bordeaux

By train

The TGV links Paris-Montparnasse to Bordeaux-Saint-Jean in just over two hours. From the station, tram C runs to Place de la Bourse.

By air

Bordeaux-Mérignac airport is linked to the centre by tram A.

Getting around

The centre is walkable; tram B serves the Chartrons and the Cité du Vin.

Tips for visiting Bordeaux

  • Come to the Water Mirror at dusk, when the square lights up.
  • Allow at least two hours for the Cité du Vin.
  • Keep half a day for Saint-Émilion, less than an hour away.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Bordeaux?

Two days are enough for the centre, the quays and the Cité du Vin; add a day for Saint-Émilion or the Arcachon Bay.

Is the Water Mirror free?

Yes, it is open access, like the quays.

How long should you spend in Bordeaux?

Allow two days.

What is there to see near Bordeaux?

In this guide, the closest places are Saint-Émilion (about 42 km away), Dune du Pilat (about 73 km away), Périgueux (about 136 km away).
