Saint-Émilion, the wine village

A medieval village of golden stone, a church carved into the rock and vineyards as far as the eye can see: Saint-Émilion is the finest day trip from Bordeaux.

Perched above its vineyards about forty kilometres east of Bordeaux, Saint-Émilion gave its name to one of the world’s most famous wines. Its jurisdiction was the first wine landscape listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, in 1999.

The village is explored on foot along steep lanes paved with big stones; its greatest treasure is underground, in the Monolithic Church.

A bit of history

The village is named after Émilion, a Breton monk who is said to have retired to a cave here in the 8th century, which became his hermitage. His followers then carved the Monolithic Church out of the limestone, one of the largest in Europe.

In 1199 King John of England granted the town a charter that gave birth to the Jurade, which still watches over its wines. In 1999 the Saint-Émilion jurisdiction became the first wine landscape listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Things to see in Saint-Émilion

The Monolithic Church

A huge church carved out of the limestone in the Middle Ages, below the bell tower that dominates the village; it is visited with the hermitage and catacombs. See tours and wine tastings in Saint-Émilion

The Tour du Roy

A 13th-century keep whose top offers the best view of the rooftops and vines.

The Grandes Murailles

The tall Gothic wall of a former convent, standing alone among the vines.

The Cordeliers cloister

A ruined cloister where sparkling crémant is made in underground galleries.

The wine châteaux

Dozens of estates around the village offer cellar tours and tastings, often by reservation.

How to get to Saint-Émilion

From Bordeaux by train

Regional TER trains link Bordeaux-Saint-Jean to Saint-Émilion station in about forty minutes; the village is then a twenty-minute uphill walk.

By car

Allow about 50 minutes from Bordeaux; car parks are at the foot of the village.

On a tour

Day trips from Bordeaux often combine the village with two châteaux.

Practical info 2026: Saint-Émilion

  • Monolithic Church: guided tours only, about 45 minutes, to be booked at the tourist office on Place des Créneaux.
  • Tour du Roy: shorter hours in winter (weekend and public holiday afternoons from February to early April); check with the tourist office.

Tips for visiting Saint-Émilion

  • Book the Monolithic Church tour as soon as you arrive, as it sells out in season.
  • Wear good shoes: the cobbles are slippery.
  • Book your château tastings in advance.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit Saint-Émilion without a car?

Yes: TER trains link Bordeaux to Saint-Émilion station, about twenty minutes’ walk from the village.

Can you visit the Monolithic Church on your own?

No, only on a guided tour, with tickets sold at the tourist office.

How long should you spend in Saint-Émilion?

Allow half a day to a day.

What is there to see near Saint-Émilion?
