La Roque-Gageac, between cliff and river

A row of golden houses squeezed below a cliff on the banks of the Dordogne: one of the most beautiful villages in France, best seen from the water.

La Roque-Gageac stretches along a narrow strip of land between the Dordogne and a tall limestone cliff. Facing due south and sheltered from the wind, it enjoys a microclimate that lets palm trees and bamboo grow in its lanes.

Listed among the Most Beautiful Villages of France, it has fewer than 500 inhabitants but draws crowds in summer for its gabarre boat trips and canoeing.

A bit of history

Once owned by the bishops of Sarlat, La Roque-Gageac was fully fortified, with its fort carved into the cliff serving as a refuge and lookout. During the Hundred Years’ War its position made it almost impregnable.

The village long lived from the river: gabarres stopped here to carry timber, wine and goods down to Bordeaux. It has been a protected site since 1943.

Things to see in La Roque-Gageac

The cliff fort

A former defensive post built into the cliff, about forty metres above the village, with a dizzying view of the valley. See gabarre boat trips and canoeing on the Dordogne

Gabarre boat trips

Replicas of the flat-bottomed boats that once carried goods on the Dordogne, for a commented trip of about an hour.

Canoeing on the Dordogne

Several outfitters offer trips down to Beynac and Castelnaud, on a calm river in summer.

Lanes and the exotic garden

Stepped lanes climbing to the church and a garden of Mediterranean and tropical plants.

Manoir de Tarde

A noble house with a round tower clinging to the rock, one of the most photographed in the village.

How to get to La Roque-Gageac

From Sarlat

About 15 minutes by car.

Parking

Pay car parks along the river; they fill up fast in summer.

Tips for visiting La Roque-Gageac

  • Take the boat trip in the morning or late afternoon, when the light is best.
  • Look at the village from the opposite bank for the best photo.
  • Combine with Beynac, Castelnaud and Marqueyssac, all minutes away.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a gabarre boat trip in La Roque-Gageac?

From €12.50 per adult and €9.50 per child, for about an hour on the Dordogne.

Can you go canoeing in La Roque-Gageac?

Yes, several outfitters offer trips down the Dordogne, notably towards Beynac and Castelnaud.

How long should you spend in La Roque-Gageac?

Allow two to three hours.
