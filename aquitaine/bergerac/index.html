Bergerac, gateway to the Périgord Pourpre

A half-timbered old town on the Dordogne, traditional boats on the river and vineyards all around: Bergerac is as much for tasting as for sightseeing.

The second town of the Dordogne, Bergerac lies on the right bank of the river in the Périgord Pourpre, home of Bergerac and Monbazillac wines. It has been a Town of Art and History since 2013.

The old quarter, between the old port and Notre-Dame church, takes half a day on foot; round it off with a boat trip or a tasting in the vineyards.

A bit of history

In the Middle Ages Bergerac grew up around its bridge over the Dordogne, one of the few on the river, and its port, from which gabarre boats carried wine down to Bordeaux and on to England. During the Hundred Years’ War the town changed hands several times between the French and the English.

In the 16th century it became a Protestant stronghold, and the Peace of Bergerac, one of the edicts meant to end the Wars of Religion, was signed here in 1577. As for Cyrano, he never lived here: the town adopted him much later.

Things to see in Bergerac

The old town

Cobbled streets lined with half-timbered houses, Place de la Myrpe, Place Pélissière and Rue Saint-Clar. See tours and wine tastings in Bergerac

The Cyrano statues

Edmond Rostand’s hero never lived here, but the town adopted him: a stone statue on Place de la Myrpe and a colourful one on Place Pélissière, the most photographed.

The Tobacco Museum

Housed in the early 17th-century Maison Peyrarède, it recalls that the valley was long a major tobacco-growing area.

The old port and the gabarres

From Quai Salvette, replicas of the boats that once carried wine and timber down to Bordeaux take visitors on the river.

Monbazillac

A few kilometres away, Monbazillac castle, built around 1550, overlooks the vineyards of the famous sweet wine; the visit ends with a tasting.

How to get to Bergerac

By train

TER trains link Bordeaux to Bergerac in just over an hour.

By air

Bergerac-Dordogne-Périgord airport has flights, mainly in season, from the UK and Belgium.

By car

About 1 h 30 from Bordeaux and 1 h 15 from Sarlat.

Practical info 2026: Bergerac

  • Bergerac gabarres: trips of about 50 minutes from Quai Salvette, €12.50 per adult and €9.50 for under-12s, from April to October.
  • Monbazillac castle: listed historic monument, tasting included at the end of the visit; prices depend on the ticket, free for under-6s.

Tips for visiting Bergerac

  • Come on a Wednesday or Saturday morning for the market around Notre-Dame church.
  • Take the boat trip late in the day for the light on the town.
  • Keep half a day for Monbazillac and the vineyards.

Frequently asked questions

Did Cyrano de Bergerac live in Bergerac?

No, the real Cyrano was a Parisian; the town adopted Edmond Rostand’s character and dedicated two statues to him.

How much is a gabarre boat trip in Bergerac?

€12.50 per adult and €9.50 for under-12s, for about 50 minutes on the Dordogne.

How long should you spend in Bergerac?

Allow half a day, a day with Monbazillac.

What is there to see near Bergerac?

In this guide, the closest places are Périgueux (about 52 km away), Saint-Émilion (about 63 km away), Beynac castle (about 65 km away).
