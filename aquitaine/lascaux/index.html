Lascaux, the world’s most famous painted cave

Closed to the public since 1963, the Lascaux cave can now be seen at Lascaux IV, a complete replica of its horses, aurochs and stags painted some 18,000 years ago.

The Lascaux cave lies on the hill above Montignac in the Vézère valley, Dordogne. Its paintings and engravings, among the most famous of prehistory, have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979 along with the other sites in the valley.

To protect it, the original cave is closed. Visitors go to Lascaux IV, the International Centre for Cave Art, opened in December 2016 at the foot of the hill: it reproduces the entire cave down to the millimetre.

A bit of history

The cave was discovered on 12 September 1940 by four teenagers from Montignac who had set out to explore a hole on the hill. They found hundreds of animal figures painted and engraved by Palaeolithic people some 18,000 years ago.

Opened to the public after the war, the cave drew so many visitors that their breath and the lighting damaged the walls, and it was closed in 1963. A first replica, Lascaux II, opened in 1983, followed in 2016 by Lascaux IV, which reproduces the whole cave.

Things to see at Lascaux

The cave replica

The heart of the visit: the Hall of the Bulls, the Axial Gallery, the Nave and the Shaft, reproduced identically in cool half-light. See tickets and trips to Lascaux

The Lascaux workshop

Panels reproduced at eye level to look closely at the painters’ techniques: blown pigments, finger tracing, engravings.

The viewpoint

The visit starts on the roof of the building, with views over the Vézère valley and the hill hiding the real cave.

Lascaux II

The first replica, opened in 1983 close to the original cave; it can be combined with Lascaux IV on a joint ticket.

Montignac

The small town on the Vézère, with its old houses and terraces, a quarter of an hour’s walk from the site.

How to get to Lascaux

By car

About 30 minutes from Sarlat and 50 minutes from Périgueux; on-site parking.

By train

The nearest stations are Condat-Le Lardin and Sarlat; you then need a taxi or a car.

On a tour

Day trips from Sarlat or Bordeaux often include Lascaux IV.

Tips for visiting Lascaux

  • Book your slot ahead in summer and during school holidays.
  • Bring a light jumper: the replica is kept cool, like a real cave.
  • Arrive twenty minutes before your slot.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit the real Lascaux cave?

No, it has been closed to the public since 1963 to protect the paintings; visitors see its replicas, Lascaux IV and Lascaux II.

How much is entry to Lascaux IV?

€23 from age 13, €15 for ages 5 to 12 and free for under-5s.

How long should you spend at Lascaux?

Allow about 2 hours.

What is there to see near Lascaux?
