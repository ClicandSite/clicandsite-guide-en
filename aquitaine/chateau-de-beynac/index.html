Beynac castle stands on the edge of a dizzying cliff above the Dordogne. Remarkably well preserved, it traces five centuries of change, from 12th-century fortress to lordly residence.

Its condition has made it a sought-after film set; below, the village of Beynac-et-Cazenac tumbles down to the river.

A bit of history

Built in the 12th century, Beynac castle controls the Dordogne valley from its cliff. During the Hundred Years’ War the Dordogne was a frontier: Beynac was French, while Castelnaud, just opposite, was held by lords allied to the English.

The Beynac family died out in the 18th century and the fortress was gradually neglected. Listed as a historic monument in 1944 and restored since, it is often used as a film set, notably for Les Visiteurs 2 and The Last Duel.

Things to see at Beynac castle

The fortress

The keep, the Hall of the States, the chapel and the furnished rooms tell the story of a fortress turned residence. See tours and outings around Beynac

The view from the keep

From the top you look over the valley, Castelnaud castle opposite and the Marqueyssac gardens.

The village of Beynac-et-Cazenac

Very steep cobbled lanes running down from the castle to the Dordogne.

Beynac gabarres

Traditional boat trips at the foot of the village.

How to get to Beynac castle

From Sarlat

About 15 minutes by car.

Parking

There is a car park at the top of the village, about fifty metres from the castle, and others by the river.

Tips for visiting Beynac castle

  • Allow 1 h 30 to 2 hours for the visit.
  • Wear closed shoes: the keep’s stairs are steep.
  • In summer, come at opening time or late afternoon.
