Cahors and its Pont Valentré

A medieval town curled inside a loop of the Lot, guarded by the most famous fortified bridge in France.

Cahors, the main town of the Lot, sits on a peninsula formed by a meander of the river. Its old town, lanes and cathedral can be explored on foot in half a day.

But the Pont Valentré is what makes it famous: a fortified medieval bridge with three square towers, almost untouched for seven centuries.

A bit of history

The Pont Valentré was built in the 14th century to defend the town. Legend says its architect made a pact with the devil to finish the work: a small devil, carved during the 19th-century restoration, still clings to the central tower.

The bridge is a Unesco World Heritage site as part of the Routes of Santiago de Compostela, which pass through the town.

What to see in Cahors

The Pont Valentré

Walk across it, then look at it from the banks of the Lot, especially late in the day. Look for the little devil on the middle tower.

Saint-Étienne cathedral

A domed cathedral with a Gothic cloister and a carved Romanesque doorway.

The old town

Medieval lanes, half-timbered houses and brick façades around the Badernes and Soubirous quarters.

Mont Saint-Cyr

A viewpoint above the town, with the whole loop of the Lot below.

Cahors wine

The vineyards, mostly Malbec, surround the town: many estates offer tours and tastings.

How to get to Cahors

By train

Cahors is on the Paris-Austerlitz – Toulouse line. The station is a few minutes’ walk from the centre.

By car

On the A20 motorway, between Brive and Montauban. Park near the Allées Fénelon or along the river.

From Rocamadour and Saint-Cirq-Lapopie

Saint-Cirq-Lapopie is about 30 minutes by road, Rocamadour just over an hour.

Practical info 2026: Cahors

  • Pont Valentré: free access, all year.
  • Cathedral: free entry outside services.
  • Market: market on the cathedral square, notably on Saturday mornings.

Information checked in November 2026; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Cahors

  • See the Pont Valentré early in the morning or at sunset, when the stone turns golden.
  • Wear good shoes for the climb to Mont Saint-Cyr.
  • Combine your visit with Saint-Cirq-Lapopie and the Pech Merle cave, less than an hour away.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Pont Valentré free?

Yes, you can walk across the bridge freely, all year.

How long do you need to visit Cahors?

Half a day is enough for the bridge, the cathedral and the old town; allow a full day with the vineyards.

How do you get to Cahors without a car?

By train: Cahors is on the Paris – Toulouse line.
