Pech Merle cave, at Cabrerets between the Lot and Célé valleys, is one of the few painted caves in France still open to the public with its original paintings rather than a replica.

The guided visit follows a gallery more than a kilometre long, among rock formations and prehistoric drawings. The ticket also covers the Amédée-Lemozi prehistory museum, with a film and an exhibition on prehistoric life in the Quercy.

A bit of history

The cave was discovered in 1922 by two teenagers from Cabrerets. Its most famous work, the panel of spotted horses, is dated to around 29,000 years ago.

There is also a black frieze of mammoths and bison, hand stencils and human footprints left in the clay.

What to see in Pech Merle cave

The spotted horses

Two horses covered in black dots, surrounded by hand stencils: one of the best-known images of prehistoric art.

The black frieze

Mammoths, bison and horses drawn in black outline on a single wall.

The footprints

Human footprints preserved in the clay floor of the cave.

The Amédée-Lemozi museum

A film and an exhibition on the prehistoric people of the Quercy, included in the ticket.

How to get to Pech Merle cave

By car

Cabrerets is about 35 minutes from Cahors along the Lot valley. Parking on site.

From Saint-Cirq-Lapopie

The village is a few kilometres away: you can easily combine both in a day.

Without a car

The nearest station is Cahors, about 35 km away: you then need a taxi or a driver.

Practical info 2026: Pech Merle cave

  • Visit: guided only, there is no self-guided visit.
  • Booking: strongly advised all year on the official website, pechmerle.com.
  • Season: open from April to early November in 2026; closed in winter outside school holidays.
  • Ticket: one ticket covers the cave and the museum.

Information checked in November 2026 with the Pech Merle Prehistory Centre and the tourist office; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Pech Merle cave

  • Book your slot ahead: daily visitor numbers are limited to protect the paintings.
  • Bring a warm layer, it is cool underground even in summer.
  • Photography is not allowed in the cave.
  • Arrive a little early to see the museum film before your visit.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to book Pech Merle cave?

Yes, booking is strongly advised all year, and visits are guided only.

Are the paintings original?

Yes, unlike Lascaux or Chauvet, here you visit the real painted cave.

When is the cave open?

In 2026, from April to early November. Check the dates on pechmerle.com.
