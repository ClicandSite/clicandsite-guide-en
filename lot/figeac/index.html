Figeac, Champollion’s town

A medieval centre of pale sandstone on the Célé, and the birthplace of the man who deciphered hieroglyphs.

By the Clic&Site team · Updated 15 November 2026

Figeac, in the east of the Lot, has one of the best-preserved medieval centres in south-west France: arcaded merchants’ houses, soleilhos (open lofts under the roofs) and lanes around the abbey.

It is also the birthplace of Jean-François Champollion, who deciphered Egyptian hieroglyphs. His house is now the World Writing museum.

A bit of history

Champollion was born in Figeac in 1790. The museum in his birthplace traces the history of writing around the world, from clay tablets to printing.

Figeac is also a stop on the Way of St James from Le Puy-en-Velay: you meet pilgrims all through the warmer months.

What to see in Figeac

The Champollion museum

The World Writing museum, in Champollion’s birthplace, behind its façade covered in letters.

Place des Écritures

A small cobbled square with a giant copy of the Rosetta Stone carved in granite.

The medieval centre

The Hôtel de la Monnaie, the houses with soleilhos and the Place du Marché and Place Carnot.

Notre-Dame-du-Puy

A church on the heights, with a view over the town’s rooftops.

The banks of the Célé

A riverside walk at the foot of the old town.

How to get to Figeac

By train

Figeac has a station, with links to Brive, Rodez and Toulouse among others.

By car

About 1 h 10 from Cahors and 50 minutes from Rocamadour. Car parks around the centre.

On foot

The centre is easy to explore on foot; part of it is pedestrianised.

Practical info 2026: Figeac

  • Champollion museum: paid entry; booking advised for guided tours. Opening times on musee-champollion.fr.
  • Medieval centre: free access.
  • Market: big market on Saturday mornings.

Information checked in November 2026 with the Grand-Figeac tourist office; it may change, so check before you go.

Tips for visiting Figeac

  • Come on a Saturday morning for the market, one of the liveliest in the Lot.
  • Look up: the soleilhos and Gothic windows are on the upper floors.
  • Combine with the Célé valley and Pech Merle cave.

Frequently asked questions

What can you see in Figeac in a day?

The Champollion museum, the Place des Écritures, the medieval centre and Notre-Dame-du-Puy.

Why is Figeac linked to Champollion?

Jean-François Champollion, who deciphered hieroglyphs, was born there in 1790.

Can you get to Figeac by train?

Yes, the town has a station, with links to Brive, Rodez and Toulouse.
